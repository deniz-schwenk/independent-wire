Saudi Arabia Forms Red Sea Defense Coalition as Trump and Tehran Split Over Negotiation Claims

A 14-nation Gulf-led alliance takes shape without the UAE and Oman, while Trump insists talks with Iran are underway and Tehran says it speaks only to Oman about Hormuz.

Saudi Arabia has formed a 14-nation Red Sea defense coalition to counter Houthi shipping threats, with the UAE and Oman not signing the founding declaration despite both being Gulf Cooperation Council members [34][39][40][47]. The alliance was established after Houthi attacks on commercial vessels and threats to impose shipping tolls modeled on Iran's fee structure in the Strait of Hormuz [34]. Yemeni Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani warned that Houthi plans to impose tolls would turn a strategic maritime corridor into militia funding, and Yemeni Foreign Minister-Designate Afrah al-Zouba stated that the Houthis want to copy the Iranian model of charging tolls [34]. Turkey has joined the coalition, BBC Turkish reported [47].

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman reportedly lobbied President Donald Trump to cancel a planned massive strike on Iran and prioritize dialogue, with multiple outlets reporting that MBS warned an escalation could severely damage the regional and global economy [2][4][13][17][39]. Trump stated that Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, and Iran all asked him to call off the attack, which he described as the largest since World War II [16][17]. Iran's diplomatic efforts with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan are ongoing alongside its negotiations with Oman over the Strait of Hormuz [33].

Trump repeatedly insisted that talks with Iran are ongoing, calling Iranian leadership "unbelievably duplicitous" for denying discussions while allegedly requesting them, and framing the moment as Iran's "last chance" before "decapitation" [4][5][6][13][27][29]. He outlined a two-phase plan covering the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and denuclearization [13][17][22]. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei denied any negotiations with the United States, stating that Iran is conducting talks exclusively with Oman on a temporary shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz [1][2][3][5][7][13][23][35]. Baghaei said a deal on a new route does not equate to reopening the strait, and that the strait will not return to its pre-war status as long as the US blockade continues [5][35][41]. Anonymous Iranian negotiating team sources called Trump's claim of an imminent deal "simplemente es falso" (simply false) [16].

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian described the June memorandum of understanding with the US as the "centre of gravity" of Iran's future foreign relations and called for the US to be compelled to honor what it signed, while parliamentary security spokesman Hassan Ghashghavi said mediators are trying to revive the MoU [2][7][13][28]. Iran's Assembly of Experts warned that "hope for understanding and agreement with this bullying regime will lead nowhere," and Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi demanded a northern route favorable to Iran while rejecting shared traffic or third-country demining [13].

Oil prices fell following Trump's announcement of talks, with Brent crude dropping between 5 and 7 percent to approximately $83 per barrel and West Texas Intermediate falling over 6 percent [6][16][19][26][32]. Energy advisory firm Ritterbusch and Associates characterized the selloff as "another overreaction to Trump's comments" [26]. The economic consequences extend beyond oil markets: Côte d'Ivoire has spent nearly 200 billion FCFA to cushion consumers from price spikes [45], Uzbekistan has faced 20 percent longer delivery times and 10-20 percent higher logistics costs [46], and Thailand, which imports about 85 percent of its oil from the Middle East, has been assessing its 60-day fuel reserves [44]. Kpler data cited by the BBC showed a 28 percent drop in commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz [4].

US military leadership is exploring expanded options against Iran. CENTCOM is seeking "creative and unconventional" pressure methods, according to an internal email reported by Turkey's Hurriyet, and Joint Chiefs Chairman Dan Caine acknowledged that air power has its limits [31]. CENTCOM reported redirecting 44 commercial vessels since mid-July as part of its naval blockade enforcement [21]. US lawmakers from both parties expressed unease about further military action, with Democratic Senator Mark Kelly calling Trump's approach "erratic" and Republican Senator John Kennedy arguing for tightening sanctions rather than risking higher energy prices [17].

Israel maintains independent red lines on Iran's nuclear and missile programs. Energy Minister Eli Cohen stated that Israel will strike Iran "with or without an agreement" if Tehran attempts to renew its nuclear program or advance its ballistic missile industries [33]. Israeli Prime Minister's Office spokesperson Doron Spielman said the publicly released peace framework "does not reflect Israel's positions" [6][27]. Former Israeli Defense Minister Avigdor Lieberman opposed the emerging US-Saudi civilian nuclear deal, arguing it would spark a regional arms race [42].

Amnesty International documented a rise in politically motivated executions in Iran since the war began, with regional director Heba Morayef stating that authorities are "unleashing a horrifying wave of executions" to suppress dissent and project strength [15][38]. Iran's judiciary, through its media outlet Mizan, defended the executions of two men as punishment for transmitting sensitive military coordinates to Mossad [6][27].

UN Secretary-General António Guterres expressed concern about regional spillover, with deputy spokesperson Farhan Haq stating that current developments "are not positive" and calling for a cessation of fighting and return to diplomatic efforts including mediation by Pakistan and Qatar [15]. BBC Persian reported that Pakistan's mediation role is largely confined to relaying messages due to lack of trust between the parties, with analysts describing a "no war, no peace" equilibrium, even as China pushes Pakistan to accelerate mediation to protect its energy interests [43].

Legal scholars at the Brookings Institution argued that the administration's claim that a ceasefire paused the 60-day War Powers Resolution clock is legally unsound, citing the ongoing naval blockade and continued exchanges of fire as constituting hostilities [48]. International law experts warned that strikes on Iranian civilian infrastructure, including schools, hospitals, and UNESCO heritage sites documented by BBC Persian, may violate international law [37]. US Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth said an explosion at a school in Minab is under investigation and stated that US forces never intentionally target civilians [37].

Washington Institute analysts assessed that Iran is pursuing a strategy of controlled escalation, leveraging its capacity to damage regional economies as strategic leverage over Washington, particularly ahead of US midterm elections [20][36].

The Strait of Hormuz remains the central sticking point across all diplomatic tracks, with Oman's proposal for a joint regional mechanism under negotiation and Iran's counterproposal demanding supervisory authority and fee collection [10][36]. A cargo vessel was struck by an unknown projectile off Oman's coast, the UK Maritime Trade Operations office reported [5][6][27].

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
