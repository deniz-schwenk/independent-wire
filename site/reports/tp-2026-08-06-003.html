Israel Issues First Lebanon Evacuation Order Since June Ceasefire and Rejects US-Backed Gaza Withdrawal Plan

Strikes on southern Lebanon coincide with Rome peace talks, while Netanyahu insists Hamas disarm before any Israeli withdrawal from Gaza.

Israel's military issued its first evacuation order for southern Lebanon since the June ceasefire framework, ordering residents of Mansouri to move north and conducting strikes on Tibnin that killed at least one person and wounded twelve, according to Lebanese health officials [1][4][28]. The Israel Defense Forces stated the strikes were a response to a blatant ceasefire violation by Hezbollah, with IDF Arabic-language spokesperson Ella Waweya telling Mansouri residents: "In light of Hezbollah's violations of the ceasefire agreement, the Israel Defense Forces are compelled to act against it with force. For your safety, you must evacuate your homes immediately and move north" [6][7][13]. The strikes coincided with US-brokered talks between Israel and Lebanon in Rome, where a senior Lebanese official said the session was unlikely to yield further Israeli pullbacks as the two sides remained split over disarming Hezbollah [20]. Israeli delegation head Yechiel Leiter requested the day's talks end early, citing what he described as misleading leaks from the Lebanese side, while a US State Department official said discussions would resume Thursday [13][21]. Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem rejected the Rome talks, saying direct negotiations would bring Lebanon "nothing but shame, humiliation" and successive concessions [1][6][8]. Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam countered that "the greatest help to Israel was provided by those who unilaterally drew Lebanon into the adventures of futile wars" [8][24]. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani spoke with his Iranian counterpart, calling on Tehran to halt Hezbollah's operations and voicing hope the talks would yield concrete results [6][9]. A Haaretz investigation reported that the Israeli military is establishing a durable presence in southern Lebanon, constructing at least seven new military bases connected by logistical roads extending up to twelve kilometers inside Lebanese territory, creating a de facto security zone of nearly six hundred square kilometers [16]. Meanwhile, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu publicly rejected the US-backed Gaza withdrawal plan, stating that Israel did not approve the draft sent by President Donald Trump's team and insisting that Israeli forces would not withdraw from their current positions until Hamas is completely disarmed [12][15][25][33]. "The Trump administration sent us a draft. We did not agree. It is not our draft. We sent our comments," Netanyahu said [25][33]. Hamas countered that it would not surrender its weapons until Israel withdraws its forces and halts attacks, creating a deadlock over sequencing [15][18][40]. The Board of Peace, the international body created by Trump to oversee Gaza's post-war framework, issued a statement conditioning Israeli withdrawal beyond the "Yellow Line" on completed Hamas decommissioning — a reversal of its earlier position linking withdrawal and disarmament to a parallel, phased process [12][17]. Analysts attributed Netanyahu's posture to pressure from far-right coalition partners ahead of October elections, with National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir dismissing the deal as unacceptable and insisting that operations in Gaza must continue [22][27][35]. US lawmakers from both parties expressed skepticism about the plan's viability, with Senator Mike Rounds (R-SD) saying "I'll believe it when I actually see it happening" and Representative Josh Gottheimer (D-NJ) noting Hamas's "long record of lying" [32]. Arab states including Jordan, Saudi Arabia, and the R-4 Group of Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, and Turkiye condemned Israeli violations in Gaza and East Jerusalem, calling for full implementation of the peace plan and a political path to a Palestinian state [3][23][36]. UN Deputy Relief Chief Joyce Msuya warned the Security Council that famine in Gaza is a human-made catastrophe, as Gaza's Health Ministry reported the overall death toll exceeding 73,377 Palestinians [25][34].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.

Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
