CIA Director's Secret Moscow Visit Emerges as Potential Pivot in Russia-Ukraine Diplomatic Track

Ratcliffe met the FSB chief and floated a leaders' summit; as strikes killed civilians on both sides, European officials called the Russian threat to the continent acute and Global South economies absorbed rising fuel costs.

CIA Director John Ratcliffe traveled unannounced to Moscow to meet FSB head Aleksandr Bortnikov, delivering a bleak assessment of Russia's war effort and pressing the Kremlin toward negotiations, according to reports citing the New York Times [35]. CNN reported Ratcliffe also warned Russia against staging an attack on NATO territory and urged Moscow to cut support for Iran [34]. Axios, via Jiji Press, reported that Ratcliffe proposed a US-Russia-Ukraine three-leader summit during the visit and that US officials briefed Zelensky on the proposal [40]. German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius drew public attention to the visit, asking in a Bundestag address why CIA Director Ratcliffe had traveled to Moscow and stating it was "certainly not to buy matryoshka dolls or go to the ballet" [10].

The intelligence channel sits alongside the public envoy shuttle of Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, whose weekend visits to Moscow and Kyiv were assessed positively by Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov in a one-hour phone call between Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump described as "constructive and frank" [3][30]. Ushakov said Trump emphasised bringing the conflict to a swift end, which would open prospects for the full restoration of US-Russia relations, and that Putin denied any aggressive plans toward Europe [16][30]. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov stated that Washington now recognizes "realities on the ground" in all contacts with Moscow, framing this as a basis for continued talks [37]. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said no formal roadmaps exist but expressed hope for a near-term resumption of trilateral negotiations, responding to US Secretary of State Marco Rubio's suggestion that a roadmap could emerge in coming weeks [29]. Ushakov also floated a possible Putin-Trump-Xi meeting at the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November [48].

Skeptics of the diplomatic track dispute that any progress was made. Bloomberg sources, cited by Ukrinform, reported that Kushner and Witkoff made no significant progress, that Putin showed no willingness to soften his position and continues to seek control of the Donbas, and that serious negotiations are unlikely before at least 2027 [20]. The same sources said Russia plans to intensify strikes on Ukraine's energy and heating infrastructure over the next six months [20]. US Under Secretary of War for Policy Elbridge Colby told the Ukraine Defense Contact Group that allies should prepare for the possibility of a prolonged war [20]. DW News reported that the Putin-Trump call and envoy shuttle yielded nothing beyond sporadic prisoner swaps [6].

Putin's assurance to Trump that Russia has no hostile plans toward European NATO states contrasts with assessments from European officials. Pistorius told the Bundestag the Russian threat is "real, serious and acute," citing the attempted drone attack on Leipzig Airport [10][19]. Moldova's President Maia Sandu warned that Russia's frontline is "moving further west" after a Russian strike on the Moldovan-Ukrainian border and repeated drone incursions into Moldovan airspace [10]. Moldova's Prime Minister Vasile Tofan said 17 drones entered Moldovan territory in August alone [4]. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte said he will urge all states to increase support for Ukraine, stating there is no alternative [19]. EU official Cosmin Dobran warned that North Korea-Russia military cooperation is structural and likely to continue beyond the Ukraine war, with signs that North Korean troop presence could increase [21].

As the diplomatic track continued, strikes killed civilians on both sides. Russian drone and missile attacks across Ukraine on September 8-9 hit residential buildings in Kyiv, a shopping centre in Sumy [4], a market in Odesa's suburbs, and passenger trains [26][32]. Strikes on September 8 killed at least five people and wounded 70 [26], and further attacks on September 9 killed at least 12 and injured 72 [32]. A Russian drone strike on the Starokozache border crossing with Moldova killed two civilians and wounded three [4][12]. The UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission reported that July 2026 was the deadliest month for civilians in Ukraine since spring 2022, with 437 killed and 2,610 injured [32][46]. Zelensky said Russia deliberately calculated the attacks to cause maximum loss of life and said the international response has been too small, warning that Moscow must not enter the winter believing it can commit missile and drone terror without consequences [32].

Russian officials described Ukrainian strikes as terrorism against civilians. Krasnodar region governor Veniamin Kondratyev said a Ukrainian drone attack on Novorossiysk killed four people including a child and wounded 29, damaging a kindergarten, an Orthodox church, and residential buildings, and said the enemy attacks "exclusively civilian targets" [22][31]. Russia's Investigative Committee opened a criminal case under the terrorism article [38]. A Ukrainian naval drone strike on the Sochi seafront injured eight people [1]. Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces shot down 596 Ukrainian drones overnight across eleven regions [31].

Ukraine's leadership describes its deep strikes as a cost-imposition strategy to force Russia into negotiations. Ukraine's Special Operations Forces announced their deepest strike of the war, hitting the Novy Urengoy gas condensate treatment plant in Siberia's Yamalo-Nenets region, over 3,200 km from Ukraine's border, stating the area had been considered "completely safe territory for the aggressor" [14]. El País reported that Ukrainian drones struck energy facilities in the Khanty-Mansi region, which produces over 40 percent of Russia's crude, with drone manufacturer Fire Point claiming a 3,300-km hit [8]. Ukrainian drones also struck the Novorossiysk naval base and oil terminal, with Ukraine's military intelligence agency reporting the frigate Admiral Essen was burning [14]. Zelensky pledged Ukraine would launch at least 1,000 drones against Russia each day [2]. Politico Europe described this as a gamble that raising the war's economic and political cost for Russians will push Putin toward serious talks faster than Russian strikes can break Ukraine [18].

Germany pledged further military aid, with Pistorius announcing urgently needed PAC-2 Patriot interceptor missiles, thousands of IRIS-T missiles financed by an EU-Ukraine defense credit, and tens of thousands of rounds of long-range artillery ammunition, warning that Russian air attacks will increase as temperatures fall [19]. The EU provided 3.9 billion euros toward a 6 billion euro drone procurement package, and the UK announced a 752 million pound package for drones and air defence missiles [2]. Ukraine's ballistic-missile interception rate fell from 24 percent in 2025 to 10.2 percent in 2026, increasing the need for Patriot and other air defence systems, according to CSIS assessments cited by Al Jazeera [2].

The war's economic spillovers continue to affect import-dependent Global South economies. Brazil's diesel price gap neared a record as Brent crude rose above $100 amid renewed Middle East conflict, prompting President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva to release R$6.6 billion in fuel subsidies [44]. Researchers and officials across Africa described fuel-price exposure and grain-and-fertilizer dependency on Russia and Ukraine as the war's chief effect on their regions, arguing for pragmatic non-alignment and supply diversification [45][47]. The DRC's Minister of Foreign Trade Julien Paluku said domestic oil refining is essential to reduce exposure to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz [47].

Peskov said Moscow hopes US-mediated talks will resume soon but will not publicly discuss settlement elements, and Rubio suggested a roadmap could emerge in the coming weeks [29]. European diplomats see a winter window for a deal, while Ukrainians regard Witkoff and Kushner as too favorable to Moscow, according to RBC-Ukraine citing the New York Times [35].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society

Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.

international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).

Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.

Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
