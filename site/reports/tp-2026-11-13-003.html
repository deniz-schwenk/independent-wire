Putin Proposes Kostiantynivka Humanitarian Mission in Moscow Talks With US Envoys, Restates Maximal Territorial Demands

The Kremlin insists the state border is the only demarcation line with Ukraine; US envoys report progress and European participation in the next round remains contested.

Russian President Vladimir Putin met US special envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner in Moscow on November 12, proposing a joint humanitarian mission to evacuate civilians from the frontline city of Kostiantynivka while restating that Russia's state border — including four annexed Ukrainian regions — is the only acceptable demarcation line with Ukraine [2][5][18]. The meeting followed two days of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi that produced agreement on what Kushner described as the war's largest prisoner exchange but no broader breakthrough [1][6][31]. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov called the Moscow talks useful and said Putin would continue Ukraine discussions with President Donald Trump at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18-19 [17][34].

Putin told the envoys that approximately 400-500 civilians remain in Kostiantynivka and proposed a five-day demining operation modeled on a prior evacuation from Oleshky, with foreign and American press invited to observe [6][18][38]. Putin thanked Trump for his mediating role and Melania Trump for her advocacy on the Oleshky operation, which she called a successful humanitarian mission that evacuated nearly 800 people and which Russian officials promoted as a humanitarian success [14][21]. Ushakov said the goal was to demonstrate who actually controls the city and that the American negotiators supported the idea [35].

Ukrainian officials rejected the premise that Russia controls Kostiantynivka. Ukraine's General Staff said Ukrainian forces continue operating against Russian infantry attempting to infiltrate the city [16][21]. The Ukrainian open-source mapping project DeepState showed the city is not under Russian control, though Russian forces advanced from Illinivka [21]. The Insider, an independent Russian outlet, used geolocation to show that Russian Defense Ministry footage purporting to depict demining in Kostiantynivka was partly filmed in other occupied settlements [36]. Ukrainian presidential adviser Dmytro Lytvyn warned that Russia used the Oleshky ceasefire to bring in equipment and rotate troops and intends the same in Kostiantynivka [5][25][33].

Putin's territorial position remained unchanged. "For us, there is only one line of demarcation: the state border," Putin said, ruling out foreign peacekeeping troops in Ukraine as a continuation of war and a legitimate target [5][13][20]. Russian delegation head Vladimir Medinsky said every inch of land where Russian soldiers have stepped belongs to Russia and that Russia will be satisfied only after victory and a peace treaty on its terms [17][23]. Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov acknowledged "undeniable" progress in the US-mediated talks but said a "very significant" gap remains between Moscow and Kyiv, and that involving Europeans could reverse gains [30].

US envoys presented a different assessment. Witkoff said progress was made, the trilateral format was restarted, and a new meeting was set with European representatives joining [6][17][19]. Kushner said the deal cannot be concluded without Europeans at the table [6][28]. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Russia is becoming more willing to accept a European seat [6][28]. Ukrainian delegation member David Arahamia said a solution before the new year is possible [23]. Anwar Gargash, diplomatic adviser to the UAE President, whose country hosted the Abu Dhabi round, expressed optimism about a breakthrough [46]. Skeptics countered that Russia publicly concedes nothing. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas accused Russia of pretending to negotiate while bombing Kyiv [11][17]. A Bloomberg-based account cited by Uzbek outlet Kun.uz described the Moscow visit as a courtesy call with no change in Russian or Ukrainian positions [15]. Romanian analyst Armand Goșu argued the three sides were not negotiating the same thing: Ukraine sought to stop the war, the US sought a deal, and Russia sought to change the subject to a Europe-wide security architecture [12].

European leaders welcomed the prospect of participation while disputing who should represent the continent. Zelensky called on EU leaders to appoint a single representative by month's end [26]. Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said European Council President António Costa and Kallas should represent Europe, arguing no new envoy is needed [37][44]. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni demanded a binding decision on who represents the EU and with what mandate [8]. Macron said "the content determines the format" and that Europeans must define their own security [11][20]. Kushner said if Europe could identify one person to speak on its behalf, that would be a greater achievement than getting Russia to include Europe [8][37].

The Kremlin conditioned European participation on Europe proving it wants peace rather than war. Peskov said current European statements do not show a desire to help move toward peace [1][45]. Ushakov said Europeans must first choose between peace and war and that their practical actions so far point to war [26]. Moscow also demanded that Europe halt military aid to Kyiv as a precondition for joining talks [26]. EU officials rejected renegotiating Europe's security architecture. Kallas said a European security architecture already exists in NATO and the EU [11][12]. Costa said there is no need to reinvent the wheel [5]. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said Europe and the US will not accept Russian demands to alter European security architecture [43]. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Poland is not welcome at the table organized by US diplomats with Putin's people and refused to endorse decisions prepared in Moscow [42].

The prospect of a Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral at APEC drew warnings from analysts and officials. Zelensky raised alarm that a Shenzhen summit could become a Yalta-like redistribution of spheres of influence [7]. Hudson Institute analyst Tsiporah Fried said the trilateral format signals that a few great powers can decide the security and future of other countries over their heads [7]. National University of Singapore political scientist Chong Ja Ian said the original Yalta opened the path to the Korean War and Indochina Wars [7]. German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier said Xi Jinping showed irritation when the idea of trilateral talks covering Ukraine came up and insisted that Europeans must be at the table [7][48]. Fudan University scholar Xin Qiang said China will not forget the lesson of great-power deals struck over its head [48].

Zelensky insisted Ukraine will not surrender Russian-occupied territory in exchange for peace [1][2]. Families of prisoners of war voiced concern that prisoner release is listed almost last in any agreement requiring concessions. Anna Lobova, wife of a severely wounded POW, said families are pained to see prisoner release relegated to the end [40]. Separately, Zelensky and the European Commission reported renewed restrictions on the Starshield satellite service degrading Ukraine's ability to intercept Russian drones, with European Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier calling for urgent restoration [5][20]. Trump denied throttling Starshield and said Elon Musk restored it [6].

Ushakov said Putin and Trump will continue discussions on all settlement-related issues at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, with work underway on a possible trilateral meeting with Xi [34]. The next round of talks is expected to include European representatives, though the format and European representation remain unresolved [3][19][26].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.

Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
