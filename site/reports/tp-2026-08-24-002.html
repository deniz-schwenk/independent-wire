Iran-Linked Hackers Shut Down UK Power Plant as US Declares "Economic D-Day"

Tehran threatens to block all Gulf oil exports if neighbors join the sanctions campaign, while touting new gas reserves and disputing reports of collapsing crude shipments.

Iran-linked hackers shut down a small-scale British power plant for four days in an attack Western reporting attributes to retaliation for UK support of US defensive operations from British bases [21][22]. The UK Department for Energy Security and Net Zero confirmed the incident but said there was no risk to the wider energy system [21]. A technical cybersecurity analysis argued the four-day outage was a deliberate demonstration of IRGC-linked capability to reach British generation assets rather than an attempt to cause harm, framing the UK attack alongside US water-sector intrusions as a coordinated campaign against Western utility infrastructure [29]. Iran International's Persian-language service reported the attack coincided with intrusions on US water infrastructure and that Iran has expanded cyber operations since February [30]. National Cyber Security Centre chief executive Richard Horne warned that hostile states including Russia, China, and Iran are increasingly targeting systems behind the UK's key services [21][22]. Conservative energy spokesperson Claire Coutinho said the incident demonstrated a new kind of warfare exploiting import dependence and called for investment in domestic gas and nuclear capacity [21][22].

The cyberattack coincided with the US announcement of what Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called an "economic D-Day" — described as the largest financial offensive ever against an adversary [18]. President Donald Trump called it "the most crushing economic operation ever taken against any country" and warned of "tremendous economic consequences" for any country providing Iran an economic lifeline [2][4][9]. Bessent told allies "you are either with us or against us" and said the goal is to sever every economic lifeline supporting the Iranian regime until Tehran is completely isolated [6][18]. El País reported that Bessent declared the objective remains to bring down the Iranian regime [8]. Trump posted an image on Truth Social labeling the Strait of Hormuz as "new US territory" [10][24].

Iran's Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei declared that any country participating in economic restrictions against Iran would be considered an enemy [1][2][6]. Rezaei outlined a three-stage strategy of negotiation, persuasion, and then punitive measures, and warned that "not a drop of oil" would pass through the Persian Gulf or the Strait of Hormuz if neighbors joined the US campaign [7][10][13][14]. He threatened to target US economic interests and alternative oil export routes beyond Hormuz [9][11][17]. Rezaei stated that Iran had breached the US naval blockade by shipping 70 million barrels of oil in recent months [10][33].

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi dismissed the sanctions as a sign of desperation, saying Washington's shift from military operations to old sanctions plans showed they are desperate and that all their actions have failed [5][9][23]. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei called the secondary sanctions an assertion of US extraterritorial sovereignty over every independent UN member state, saying they find no foundation in international law and violate the principle of sovereign equality under the UN Charter [2][4][12][20]. President Masoud Pezeshkian called for ending the war "today, when we are powerful and have dignity," while acknowledging that Iranian society faces "many difficulties" [5][9][15]. Pezeshkian said Iran cannot attract investment in the current frozen phase because capital is extremely sensitive to risk [16].

Iranian officials announced the discovery of new gas reserves in Fars province exceeding 160 billion cubic meters of extractable gas, with Petroleum Minister Mohsen Paknejad noting the gas is sweet and free of hydrogen sulfide, reducing exploitation costs [15]. State-aligned media highlighted claims of continued oil exports as evidence that sanctions and blockade have not achieved their goals [7][10][33]. However, Central Bank governor Abdolnaser Hemmati confirmed that Iran's oil exports have effectively fallen to zero and access to foreign currency has become increasingly constrained [31]. Commodity data from Kpler showed Iran's crude loadings collapsing from 893,000 barrels per day in July to 156,000 barrels per day through August 17 [43]. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright stated the US military helped move a seven-day average of 8 million barrels per day through the strait, down from more than 20 million before the war [12]. CNN reported that more than 80 percent of liquids transits through the strait over the past two weeks took the Omani route [35]. BBC Persian reported that Iran's poverty rate reached 41 percent in 2025 and is projected to hit 45.5 percent by 2027, with rural poverty potentially surpassing 50 percent by 2028 [40].

Iran researcher Hamidreza Azizi of the Clingendael Institute described Tehran's shift toward "early deterrence," where even attack preparations could trigger retaliation, as part of a new offensive doctrine of preemption and disproportionate response [10][17][19]. Armed Forces Chief of Staff Ali Abdollahi showcased underground missile production and promised "crushing, punishing and devastating responses" to any threat [12]. VnExpress reported that analysts warn an Iranian attack on a NATO member could trigger Article 4 or Article 5, potentially giving Washington an advantage in building a European coalition [25]. An analysis from Infobae described the consolidation of power by IRGC military leaders following the death of Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, with new billboards in Tehran featuring security-focused figures and transforming the Islamic Republic's character [27]. US House Speaker Mike Johnson said he expects a new phase of the Iran war soon and stated that Iranians cannot be trusted at the negotiating table [3].

The UAE suspended commercial and financial ties with Tehran [6][8][11]. France and Saudi Arabia are discussing alternative maritime routes to reduce dependence on the Strait of Hormuz, with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman traveling to Paris for talks [9][31]. Al-Monitor reported that Iran warned Gulf states it would hit their oil, power, and water plants unless they convince Trump to end strikes, and that bin Salman subsequently spoke with Trump urging a return to negotiations [32]. Egypt and Pakistan engaged in diplomatic talks with Iranian counterparts on de-escalation, with Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir planning a visit to Tehran [2][5][23]. South Korea is weighing its first-ever military contribution to Hormuz security after US pressure, while Japan is advancing alternative crude-oil procurement routes bypassing the strait [36][37]. Reports describe Iran bypassing the naval blockade via overland routes through Pakistan and Central Asia, and India moving to hand over Chabahar port operations to Iran to avoid its own banks being sanctioned [38][39]. African nations reported fuel rationing, price surges, and route disruptions tied to the Hormuz standoff, with governments in Kenya, Ethiopia, and Cape Verde taking emergency measures to protect supply and households [41].

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian said sanctions and pressure will not help resolve the issue and called for political and diplomatic means [6]. European geopolitical institute specialist Sébastien Boussois doubted sanctions would collapse the regime, saying no historical example exists [15]. Lancaster University professor Simon Mabon argued that Gulf countries cannot change geography and must live alongside Iran, suggesting some Gulf states pushed for harder strikes to weaken IRGC hardliners rather than precipitate regime collapse [19]. RBC analyst Helima Croft questioned whether more sanctions will change Iran's behavior, noting Tehran appears to believe it can outlast the US [42]. Quincy Institute executive vice president Trita Parsi said no nation in the region can afford another long war and that depleted global oil inventories make prolonged confrontation unattractive [19].

Bessent is set to announce concrete sanctions measures, with the US seeking cooperation from China, which buys more than 80 percent of Iran's shipped oil [34][12]. The 60-day negotiation window between the US and Iran lapsed without a deal [4][43]. Trump said Iran "would love to make a deal, but they're not ready to make the right deal" [5][23].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
