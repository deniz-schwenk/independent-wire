Russian Ballistic Missiles Strike Kyiv Overnight, Damaging Civilian Infrastructure, as Abu Dhabi Talks Near

Moscow says its strikes target military-industrial assets; Ukrainian officials and a UN commission call them a deliberate campaign against civilians.

November 7, 2026

Russian ballistic missiles struck Kyiv and its suburbs overnight on November 6–7, injuring at least five people in the city according to Mayor Vitali Klitschko, including a 14-year-old girl. The State Emergency Service reported three injured, including a child, and the Kyiv City Military Administration reported two. The strikes damaged residential buildings, a kindergarten, and the Northern Bridge, which was closed to traffic [10][13][17]. The State Emergency Service of Ukraine reported fires and destruction in the Solomianskyi district, while Kyiv Oblast Governor Tymur Tkachenko said a jet-powered drone strike in the Brovary district injured a man hospitalized with burns and shrapnel wounds; the service reported that a couple were injured in the same strike [17][10]. The attacks followed President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's evening warning that intelligence showed Russia preparing a new strike on energy facilities, Dnipro River bridges, and civilian infrastructure, and his plea for partners not to delay Patriot interceptor deliveries [4][8][9].

Russia's Defense Ministry stated that its strikes targeted energy facilities supplying electricity to military-industrial enterprises in Vinnytsia, Dnipropetrovsk, and Kharkiv oblasts, as well as port infrastructure in Odesa Oblast and vessels used for military purposes [20]. A subsequent Defense Ministry statement reported strikes on energy facilities, transport infrastructure used by Ukrainian forces, ammunition depots, and drone production sites across 145 districts [21]. Neither Russian statement mentioned civilian damage or casualties [20][21].

Ukrainian officials and a UN commission describe the strikes as a deliberate campaign against civilians. The UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry accused Russia of crimes against humanity over energy strikes, calling the attacks targeted and part of a state policy to deprive civilians of vital energy resources [7]. Ukraine's military intelligence service (GUR) said Russian forces knew that humanitarian aid was distributed on Mondays at the Komyshuvakha resilience centre, where residents came for food, medicine, and essential items, before striking it [2]. Zelenskyy said every Ukrainian power plant has suffered damage, including equipment essential for nuclear plant operation [5].

The energy system's condition is a central point of dispute. DTEK, Ukraine's largest private power producer, reported that several of its facilities in the Dnipropetrovsk region were struck, damaging equipment and causing a fire, with previous restorations taking up to three days [16]. The OSW Centre for Eastern Studies reported that generation was suspended at three DTEK thermal power stations and that none of the 29 ballistic and hypersonic missiles fired on November 3 were intercepted, while the Ukrainian Air Force Command, cited in the same analysis, said it shot down 49 of 59 cruise missiles and three ballistic missiles during the attack [22]. The International Energy Agency warned that power shortages are likely this winter, noting that Ukraine added around 400 MW of distributed gas-fired generation along with new wind capacity and battery storage, but that faster jet-powered Russian drones may outpace these gains [23]. At the Ibero-American Summit in Madrid, Zelenskyy appealed to Spain and Latin American countries for transformers, cables, and generators to repair Ukraine's energy system [29]. Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov said electrical disruptions remain most difficult in the capital region, where crews partially shut down the local grid [6]. Regional administrations in Dnipropetrovsk and Zhytomyr oblasts reported 200,000 and 180,000 customers without power, respectively [22].

Ukrainian officials and the International Atomic Energy Agency warn that strikes on substations critical to nuclear power plants create risks beyond Ukraine's borders. Energy Minister Denys Shmyhal called a drone attack on a substation serving the Khmelnytskyi Nuclear Power Plant a threat to nuclear safety across Europe [19]. The IAEA completed a mission assessing 19 substations key to off-site power for Ukraine's nuclear plants, days before being informed of an attack on the Slavutych substation that reportedly resulted in the loss of all its 330 kV lines [24]. The November 3 mass strike involved 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles, and 59 cruise missiles, mainly aimed at power plants and transmission substations [19].

Diplomatic efforts continue alongside the strikes. US President Donald Trump said "`Stiamo facendo molti progressi con la Russia e l'Ucraina`" (We are making a lot of progress with Russia and Ukraine), adding that both sides want a large agreement involving Europe [1]. US special envoy Steve Witkoff announced that American, Ukrainian, and Russian delegations would meet in Abu Dhabi on November 10–11 to discuss post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, and humanitarian measures [28]. The UAE has hosted 27 prisoner-exchange mediations and two previous trilateral rounds [28]. Zelenskyy called on Turkey's mediating role and said a trilateral leaders' meeting in Turkey had been discussed [31]. China's foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said "`中方将继续以自己的方式，为缓解乌克兰人道局势发挥建设性作用`" (China will continue to play a constructive role in its own way to ease Ukraine's humanitarian situation) [30].

European and Ukrainian officials argue the strikes demonstrate Russia is not pursuing peace. European Commission spokesperson Christian Wigand said Russia's missile and drone strikes, which left 600,000 people without electricity, are not the actions of a country seeking peace, and that a ceasefire cannot work if only one side observes it [26]. EU High Representative Kaja Kallas said the EU would welcome an energy ceasefire as soon as it holds, while noting uncertainty about Russian compliance [25]. Zelenskyy said Ukraine has determined its next responses to Russian energy strikes and continues its operation against Russia's oil refining industry, saying that long-range sanctions, mid-range strikes, and partner pressure create incentives for real negotiations [12]. Ukraine was reported to have struck the Saratov Oil Refinery and the Almaz military factory in Rostov, and to have targeted the Proton electronic components factory in Oryol, overnight on November 7, according to a monitoring channel; the details could not be immediately verified [14]. The Institute for the Study of War assessed that Russian territorial gains fell to 117.38 square kilometers in the first ten months of 2026, and Ukraine's General Staff estimated 381,300 Russian casualties over the same period [2].

A separate dispute has emerged between Ukraine's central government and Kyiv's municipal leadership over the capital's power outages. Zelenskyy said the outages result from unimplemented city-level decisions on heating-season preparation and Russia's operation against Ukraine's energy sector [3][11]. Mayor Vitali Klitschko rejected the accusations, saying "`Мантра центральної влади, що Київ – найгірший, постійні маніпуляції, брехня – погана історія`" (The mantra of the central government that Kyiv is the worst, constant manipulation, lies — is a bad story), and said Kyiv began supplying district heating on October 16 while city representatives were not invited to preparation consultations [11][3].

The Trump-Putin diesel arrangement adds a further dimension. Trump invited Russia on October 9 to sell 1.8 million tonnes of diesel to the global market, despite US oil sanctions and sanctions legislation he signed 11 days earlier [2]. US Senator Richard Blumenthal said "`the President may have given a licence to facilitate this transaction, but only Congress can make it legally valid`" [2]. The Kremlin dismissed reports that four diesel tankers failed to find buyers as fake news [2].

The Abu Dhabi trilateral talks are scheduled for November 10–11, with the Russian delegation led by Vladimir Medinsky and the US represented by Witkoff and Jared Kushner [27][28]. The Kremlin confirmed its delegation would attend while maintaining red lines against Ukraine joining NATO and against Western troop deployments on Ukrainian territory [27]. Zelenskyy seeks a security guarantee system lasting at least ten years and says Ukraine still lacks clear answers on how the US and Europe would respond to a future Russian attack [27].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
