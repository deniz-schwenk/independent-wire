Israeli Opposition Leader Brands West Bank Settler Arson 'Jewish Terrorism' as UN Warns Gaza Lost 77 Years of Development

Eisenkot's condemnation splits Israeli political figures while Oxfam finds 99% of Gaza environmentally contaminated and demolitions continue in southern Lebanon.

Israeli opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot condemned overnight settler arson attacks on at least four Palestinian villages in the West Bank as "Jewish terrorism in every sense," splitting Israeli political figures between those blaming the governing coalition and those calling the violence the work of a fringe minority [7][26][30]. Settlers burned approximately a dozen vehicles and several homes and spray-painted graffiti reading "Revenge" and "Death to Arabs" in Kafr Malik, Deir Abu Mash'al, and Deir al-Sudan near Ramallah [7][10][26]. Israeli police opened an investigation and dispatched forces to the affected villages [11][26]. The attacks came as an Oxfam study found more than 99% of Gaza shows indicators of war-related environmental contamination [2][28], and as Israeli forces carried out further demolitions in southern Lebanon [27].

Eisenkot, tasked by President Isaac Herzog with forming a government, blamed ministers Bezalel Smotrich and Itamar Ben-Gvir for bringing the West Bank to "unchecked anarchy" and said the arson destabilizes the region and damages Israel's national security [7][11][26]. Yair Lapid called the attacks "the product of criminal negligence by the Israeli government" [7]. Retired major general Noam Tibon warned that "if Israel does not eliminate Jewish terrorism in the West Bank, it will destroy Israel" [7]. Yair Golan said the West Bank is on the brink of explosion and warned of a third intifada [7]. Herzog condemned "violence by Jewish extremists against innocent people" as "despicable" [5].

Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar framed the attacks as the work of "a handful of extremists" contrary to Israeli values [11][26]. Ben-Gvir accused Eisenkot of selling out to Arab parties that could join a prospective coalition [26][11]. Caretaker Defence Minister Israel Katz said police, not the military, should handle violent settlers, saying the IDF "should not and cannot deal with civilian law enforcement over the Jewish population in Judea and Samaria" [5][32]. A senior settler leader called for reconsidering the use of administrative detention [33]. The IDF condemned the arson only after the fires had died down [31].

Palestinian officials and Arab Israeli lawmakers placed the settler violence within a broader pattern of occupation policy. Youssef Jabarin, head of the Joint List, said the attacks are "the direct result of the deepening occupation and a government policy under which violence against Palestinians has become an everyday occurrence" [11][26]. Al Jazeera's Arabic service reported residents describing a "terrifying night" in which fire nearly consumed them as they slept [34]. OCHA reported 40 settler attacks affecting 30 West Bank communities in one week, with more than 5,150 Palestinians displaced across the West Bank in 2026 [13]. A Physicians for Human Rights Israel study documented a 350% increase in incidents against Palestinian healthcare workers between 2022 and 2024, with report author Imran Anati calling it "a systematic assault on Palestinian emergency medical care" [25][12]. A Palestinian paramedic described a checkpoint delay in which a pregnant woman's baby died in his ambulance [12].

In Gaza, an Israeli airstrike killed at least three Palestinians near a police outpost in Khan Younis, with five wounded [3][35]. Another strike killed UNRWA worker Adel al-Helo near a water well in Jabalia camp; UNRWA said staff were engaged in regular water-well duties [4][5][16]. Al-Helo's brother Nizar said Israel does not "make any difference between the civilian people and other people, even the people of the United Nations" [4][5]. The Gaza Ministry of Health reported 1,679 Palestinians killed and 6,100 wounded since the October 2025 ceasefire [9]. Mohammed Musleh, mayor of Deir el-Balah's Maghazi municipality, said more than 500 housing units were destroyed in less than a month, describing "what appears to be a systematic policy of gradual creeping displacement" [16].

Israel maintains its Gaza campaign is self-defence after Hamas-led militants killed approximately 1,200 people and took more than 250 hostages in October 2023 [3][4][5]. An Israeli embassy spokesperson rejected the Oxfam environmental report as falling "short of professional standards," saying its satellite-imagery methodology ignores Hamas's tunnel excavation [2][18]. The spokesperson said Hamas "claimed to have excavated more than 500 km of tunnels beneath Gaza" and that a report failing to examine that scale is "fundamentally incomplete" [18].

Oxfam's Amitabh Behar called the environmental destruction "environmental war crimes" within "an ongoing genocide in Gaza" [2][18]. The study, conducted with the Conflict and Environment Observatory, found 61 million tonnes of debris containing asbestos, heavy metals, and explosive residues — a tonnage the UN Environment Programme has also estimated, with about 15% of the debris potentially high-risk contaminated [2][14][28]. Residents described living amid sewage and contaminated water; a Gaza laboratory director reported more than 400 meningitis cases in approximately one month [14]. Oxfam's Lama Abdul Samad said persistent contaminants will cause long-term chronic health impacts including respiratory disease, cancers, and birth defects [2][18]. A UN independent commission of inquiry found that Israel committed genocide in Gaza and that Israeli officials including Netanyahu incited these acts [18].

UN Deputy Secretary-General Amina Mohammed said the Gaza war has "undone 77 years of human development," citing a joint UN-World Bank assessment [15]. She called for unimpeded aid access and a Palestinian-led recovery, saying "aid must never be obstructed, diverted, or used as a bargaining chip" [15]. OCHA reported that Israeli restrictions on equipment and materials, compounded by funding shortfalls, are reversing humanitarian gains, with 1.98 million people needing shelter assistance and two-thirds of winter shelter funding unmet [29]. UN Acting Special Coordinator Ramiz Alakbarov told the Security Council that Israeli restrictions impede winter preparedness for a fourth consecutive winter [29].

Australia sanctioned three people and four Israeli entities linked to settlement expansion, and the United Kingdom imposed sanctions on eight Israeli construction companies involved in building settlements in the West Bank [7][39]. Morocco's King Mohammed VI said Gaza's reality falls short of ceasefire hopes and called for an end to settler attacks [40]. Russia demanded a halt to Israel's settlements in the West Bank [38], Iran's Foreign Ministry condemned "terrorist acts" by settlers [44], and the African Union called for an end to the killing of Palestinians [42]. A joint statement was issued by a number of Arab and Islamic countries [41].

In southern Lebanon, Israeli forces detonated explosives to destroy structures in Qantara, Zawtar al-Sharqiyah, and Kfar Tibnit [27]. Residents of Nabatieh al-Fawqa and Zawtar protested the Lebanese government's "silence" over Israel's occupation up to the Litani River [27]. Parliament Speaker Nabih Berri refused Independence Day congratulations, calling this year's independence "incomplete and unfulfilled" [27].

Hundreds of students at Israeli universities protested the administrative detention of Palestinian student Mahdi Tunsi, arrested at Hebrew University on November 8, with the peace movement Standing Together demanding due process [20]. Israeli police said the investigation concerns "links to a terrorist act" without giving details [20].

Eisenkot's coalition negotiations with Likud ended without agreement after four hours in Herzliya, with red lines over including Ben-Gvir and Likud's refusal to rotate the premiership [23]. Netanyahu rejected any coalition backed by Arab lawmakers [23]. Eisenkot threatened to form a minority emergency government if the deadlock persists [23] and is preparing a bill for a state commission to investigate Netanyahu's role before October 7 [19].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
