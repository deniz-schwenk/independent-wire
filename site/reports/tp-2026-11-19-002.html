Tankers Struck in Strait of Hormuz as Iran Attacks US Base in Kuwait and US Carrier Departs Region

One seafarer is dead and two are missing amid the shipping attacks, while US and Iranian officials trade contradictory claims over a reported dirty-bomb plot and mediators pursue a negotiated end to the nine-month war.

Multiple tankers were struck in the Strait of Hormuz in a series of attacks, leaving one crew member dead and two others missing, while Iranian drones targeted a US military installation in Kuwait and a US aircraft carrier departed the Persian Gulf region [1][19][33][35][46]. The tanker attacks came amid a cycle of reciprocal strikes that has lasted nearly nine months since the United States and Israel launched coordinated attacks on Iran in February, and the drone strike on Kuwait's Ali Al Salem Air Base followed US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island [7][10][14][31].

The Marshall Islands-flagged tanker Velos Safari, bound for Bahrain, was hit by an explosion that killed one crew member, the vessel's Greek operator Velos Tankers confirmed [46]. The Panama-flagged LPG tanker TARA GAS, carrying 24 Indian crew, was attacked twice near Khasab, Oman, leaving one sailor missing after a fire forced the crew to abandon ship [1][19][33][34]. A projectile of unknown origin also struck a tanker in the strait without causing fatalities [29]. An ADNOC Logistics vessel was struck, with the UAE-based operator reporting one crew member missing and 11 injured, and said 15 of its ships have been hit by missile and drone attacks since the war began [14][22][25]. The IRGC Navy said two US-escorted vessels struck mines and caught fire, while Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a ship carrying LNG and two oil tankers caught fire in the strait [5][41]. India's Ministry of External Affairs said the continuing attacks on shipping pose "a serious threat to the safety of civilian seafarers" and called for unimpeded navigation to be restored [33]. India's shipping minister Sarbananda Sonowal said the government is monitoring the situation and coordinating to locate the missing seafarer [1][33].

Iran's army said it launched combat and kamikaze drones at Patriot air-defense systems and US drone and missile launch platforms at the Ali Al Salem base in Kuwait [12][14][30]. Kuwait's armed forces said air defenses were repelling hostile drones and that explosion sounds across the country resulted from interceptions [22][30]. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry condemned the attack as "repeated and criminal" and a direct threat to its security and stability [25][31]. Bahrain's armed forces said they intercepted another Iranian attack, counted as the third this week in one report and the fourth since Friday in another, accusing Tehran of a "systematic hostile approach" [14][18][22]. Residents in Kuwait described renewed fear as air-defense intercepts returned to their skies [45].

Iran's Foreign Ministry cast the strikes on US-used bases in the Gulf as legitimate self-defense against US bombing of Iran's southern coast, invoking Article 3 of UN General Assembly Resolution 3314 to argue that Gulf states permitting US offensive operations from their territory are themselves committing aggression [9]. Iranian Army spokesman Brigadier General Mohammad Akrami-Nia said the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until the enemy accepts Iran's conditions and that Iran's armed forces monitor and control the strait from east to west [6]. Army chief General Amir Hatami warned of a "more devastating" riposte "without geographic limits" if Iran's territory is attacked again [14][22]. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said it "makes absolutely no difference to us whether the United States wants negotiations or war" [2]. Judiciary head Gholam Hossein Mohseni Ejei said Iran's armed forces have not retreated and will not retreat [25]. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Sarea said Houthi forces launched five drones at two pumping stations along Saudi Arabia's East-West oil pipeline, claiming "direct and precise hits" [4]. The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a Houthi drone and a cruise missile fired toward Riyadh [4][12].

US officials described the strikes on Qeshm Island as a defensive response to IRGC aggression against US soldiers, with a US military official telling Al Jazeera that American aircraft used precision munitions against an IRGC missile launch site [10][13][14]. President Donald Trump said the US has destroyed Iran's navy, air force, and air defenses, cut its missiles to roughly 12 percent of pre-war levels, and holds full control of the Strait of Hormuz [16]. Trump said he no longer considers a deal with Tehran necessary because the war has already been won and may hold off on resuming large-scale strikes if talks progress [15]. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the US does not need military strikes if economic pressure achieves the same objective [2]. Iran said it struck a US radar system at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa base, the third such attack in five days, and the United States answered with the strikes on Qeshm, which Iranian officials said caused no significant damage [4][27][32].

A CNN report citing seven unnamed US officials said Iran was developing plans to target senior Trump administration officials in Washington with a radiological dirty bomb or chemical weapon, though no decision to carry out an attack had been made [7][11]. Trump said the US takes the possibility "very seriously" and warned Iran would pay "a price the likes of which no other country has ever paid before" [5][7]. Speaking in Shenzhen, Trump said there are signs Iran is pursuing chemical and biological weapons and threatened to "completely wipe them out" [28]. US Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton told the House Homeland Security Committee that Iranian threats to Americans at home and abroad have increased sharply [3][8][23]. FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia accused Iran of espionage, cyber operations, and plots against US officials and dissidents, citing 18 Iran-related arrests [35]. The US Embassy in Bahrain warned American citizens of a heightened threat of armed attacks by Houthis and Iranian efforts to target US officials and facilities in the region [21]. However, Rubio said he does not believe there is an imminent threat to Washington, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Iran does not pose an immediate dirty-bomb threat, and White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said there are "no credible threats to the homeland" [7][23][24].

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei dismissed the dirty-bomb report as "fabricated" and accused Washington of laying media groundwork for its "sinister designs," saying the US was trying to build legitimacy "step by step" for aggressive actions [5][17][27]. Independent defence consultant Tallha Abdulrazaq said a dirty bomb would be a weapon of mass disruption rather than destruction and judged the likelihood of an Iranian attack on US soil to be infinitesimally remote [36].

Qatari, Omani, and Pakistani mediators continued diplomatic efforts to narrow the gap between Washington and Tehran [4][13][15]. Iran's Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi met Oman's Sultan Haitham bin Tariq in Muscat, briefing him on Tehran's diplomatic initiative [4]. Pakistan's Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar said Pakistan "has not given up hope" for a negotiated peace and disclosed that Tehran had presented a "two-day plan" in which the US would end wars, lift sanctions, unfreeze assets, and end the naval blockade before Iran reopens the strait and final negotiations begin [20][27]. Qatar's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Majed Al Ansari said the talks aimed at bringing positions closer and narrowing differences between the proposals [13][15]. Israel, which launched the war alongside Washington in February, continued to press for a resumption of hostilities, with Mossad Director Roman Gofman lobbying US officials for joint US-Israeli strikes [22][31].

Analysts warned that the frozen conflict is untenable. Hamidreza Azizi of the International Crisis Group said Iran may become increasingly willing to take military risks as economic pressure mounts [2]. Rouzbeh Parsi said Iran's population is "suffering tremendously" and that Trump's midterm defeat removed a restraint on the administration [2]. Mohammad Farzanegan said it is unclear how long the government can maintain stability under fragile economic conditions [2].

In the US Congress, Representative Thomas Massie was courting Republican defectors for a fourth War Powers resolution, saying he was offering colleagues "a lifeboat from MAGA" [37]. The push followed Republican losses of both chambers of Congress in November's midterms, which sources attributed in part to the war's unpopularity [7][13].

Asian and European governments moved to absorb the energy-supply shock. South Korea's government said it had secured over 70 percent of November crude volumes through reserve-oil swaps [40]. China cushioned the impact by drawing down inventories and adjusting refinery operations [38][39]. Turkey promoted itself as an alternative transit corridor, with Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar citing the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline's 1.5 million barrel capacity [42]. German economist Claudia Kemfert warned that alternative pipeline capacity remains too limited to offset a major disruption [42]. In Italy, consumers faced higher pump prices even though only 13.4 percent of the country's crude comes from the Middle East, an effect attributed to global commodity pricing [43]. In Kenya, the energy regulator raised petrol prices by 6 percent citing higher import costs tied to the conflict [47].

Bloomberg reported that a US aircraft carrier had left the Persian Gulf, with two remaining in the region [35]. Trump prepared to meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, where the Middle East war was on the agenda [14][15]. Russia's Foreign Ministry said a military scenario cannot be excluded if negotiations fail [44].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
