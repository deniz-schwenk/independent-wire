Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Resigns Over Affair With Suspected Chinese Spy, Drawing Congressional Testimony Demands and Share Declines

Huang's departure ends 33 years of leadership at the world's most valuable semiconductor company and raises questions about US export controls and global AI supply chains.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO, and board member on November 12, 2026, after the board opened an independent investigation into his relationship with Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive US intelligence suspects of collecting information for Beijing [2][24]. CFO Colette Kress was named interim CEO and former Synopsys chief Harvey Jones was appointed as the board's first chair [3][12]. Nvidia shares fell sharply at the open, with declines reported from about 7 percent to nearly 14 percent depending on the outlet and moment measured [1][5][12][19][23]; DW reported more than $600 billion wiped from Nvidia's market value in the board's Wednesday session [5], RFI put the single-day opening loss at more than $1 trillion [23], Novaya Gazeta Europe cited an $888 billion weekly loss [19], and HotNews.ro reported roughly $1 trillion in cumulative losses for the month [12]. Huang acknowledged his infidelity in a November 8 email to employees, writing: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry" [29]. He said he would cooperate fully with the board's independent investigation and was determined to protect Nvidia and support US national security [13][24]. The New York Times subsequently reported a second affair with Nvidia healthcare vice president Kimberly Powell, prompting the board to meet and discuss Huang's future [18][30].

US lawmakers across both parties demanded testimony and investigations. Senator Tim Scott requested that Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick appear before the Senate Banking Committee, saying "recent reports raise serious concerns about our national security and the protection of sensitive US technology" [2][11]. Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said Congress must require Huang and Nvidia executives to explain how they may have put US national security at risk, and that the Trump administration must account for what it did after learning of Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence agent [11][23]. House Speaker Mike Johnson said there is a growing consensus in Congress on limiting chip exports to China [4][38]. Three House committees summoned Huang for a closed-door interview on December 1 [2], and House Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast said he expected Huang to testify within a week [38].

Reporting on a memo overseen by Secretary of State Marco Rubio framed the episode as a completed intelligence failure: US intelligence flagged Alice Huang as an agent of China's Ministry of State Security roughly a year ago, but the Trump administration took no action [24][27]. Rubio reportedly warned Trump in person in May about Huang's liaison with Alice Huang, just before Huang joined a presidential delegation to Beijing [23]. Trump defended Huang, saying he had "done an amazing job for our country" and, according to El Financiero's translation of his remarks, describing him as a very honorable man [4][9]. Trump also suggested on Truth Social that Apple's Tim Cook should replace Huang, declaring "a legend replaces a legend" [6][35]. Representative Jim Himes, after a classified briefing, said he was unsure whether Alice Huang was a Chinese spy or a "useful idiot" and hoped the FBI would launch a major counterintelligence investigation [31]. The Justice Department said it had not reached any conclusion on whether Alice Huang engaged in espionage [2][34].

China rejected the espionage allegations. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun called the story "an unfounded accusation and slander" [24]. Chinese state broadcaster CGTN reported the resignation as an orderly board transition, quoting board chair Harvey Jones praising Kress's experience and Kress pledging continuity, with no mention of the spy scandal [3]. DW reported that Beijing accused the US of using the spying claims as a pretext to impose a technological blockade on China [5].

Legislators argued that export controls on advanced AI chips to China had failed and must be tightened. Senator Elizabeth Warren demanded that Commerce Secretary Lutnick and Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler explain why they had not closed export-control loopholes [11]. Senator John Kennedy said Lutnick "needs to answer that in front of God and country" and should stop selling advanced chips [11]. Maeil Business Newspaper reported that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent asked Lutnick at a White House Situation Room meeting why Megaspeed had not been added to the export control list, and Lutnick replied that he did not know and would look into it [18]. White House officials were discussing whether to replace Lutnick [34][37].

Investors and analysts warned that Huang's departure left an execution vacuum. ClearBridge portfolio manager Michael Feldman said "the risk is not that Nvidia suddenly loses its technology roadmap" but execution, because "Jensen has been the chief conductor of an extraordinarily complex system" [4][15]. JP Morgan analysts said Huang's departure "leaves a vacuum that will be difficult to fill" and that new deals could take longer or require more compensation [11]. Citi's Jon Zauderer said investors would look for alternatives such as AMD, Arista, and Broadcom [11]. Blue Bear Capital's Thomas Ferguson said he did not know whether there had ever been such a pivotal figure on whom so much of an industry rested [9][28]. Nvidia employees petitioned the board to reinstate Huang, with one posting "Nvidia is Nvidia because of Jensen" [32]. Damac founder Hussain Sajwani called Huang's departure "a big loss for the world" [41], and Saudi investor Prince Khaled bin Alwaleed called removing Huang the "stupidest" decision the board could make [41].

The board, interim CEO Kress, and other analysts stressed continuity. Nvidia said it had "no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised" [2][17]. Jones said the board expected operations, customer commitments, and the product roadmap to continue without interruption [21][41]. Kress said Nvidia's "strength has always come from its people" and committed to building on Huang's 33-year foundation [11][21]. Yorkville Ives analyst Dan Ives said Nvidia's "leading-edge GPU technology will go on" and its entrenched position in the AI chip arms race would not change [5]. Kiwoom Securities researcher Han Ji-young said Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin mass-production schedule and major customer supply contracts were largely fixed, limiting the impact on Korean business over the next one to two years [40]. Tokyo stocks rebounded on November 13, with Jiji Press reporting that Huang's resignation was largely priced in [39].

Chinese analysts judged that the political fallout would guarantee stricter controls on advanced chips and compute leasing, accelerating China's domestic semiconductor substitution [36]. CITIC Securities said Huang's resignation combined with tighter overseas restrictions may accelerate gains in domestic chip market share [36]. Gulf stakeholders assessed the bigger risk to their sovereign AI projects as tighter US export controls rather than immediate chip-supply disruption [41]. Japan's Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary Osaki Masanao said Japan would closely monitor the impact on its AI industry and semiconductor supply chains [37]. Brazilian and African outlets reported potential disruption to AI infrastructure plans using Nvidia GPUs [42][43].

European officials and executives argued the crisis exposed the continent's dependence on a single US chip supplier. European Council President António Costa asked EU leaders "how Europe can become a leading and sovereign player in strategic areas of the AI value chain" [33]. Malgorzata Nikowska, head of unit at the European Commission's AI Office, said "we cannot abandon the frontier ambition. It's an issue of our sovereignty, it's an issue of our competitiveness" [33]. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo and Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said in letters ahead of an EU meeting that Europe can compete at the frontier and that its ambition "must go beyond adoption of AI" [33].

Huang has agreed to testify before Congress by November 26 [26]. Nvidia faces a November 17 earnings test and a November 18 closed-door congressional briefing [14]. Shareholders filed a class-action lawsuit against Huang and Nvidia for failing to disclose information about the relationship to investors [17]. The board is searching internally and externally for a permanent successor, with Lisa Su, Rene Haas, and Sanjay Mehrotra floated as candidates [28].

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
