Suspected Ukrainian Strikes Hit Russian Naval Assets in Baltic and Libya as Russia Launches 186-Drone Barrage Across Ukraine

Finnish defence officials and Libyan investigators attribute the maritime attacks to Ukrainian forces; rival governments in Libya condemn the strikes as sovereignty violations.

A Russian cargo ship caught fire in the Gulf of Finland after a drone strike on November 21, and a Russian Navy corvette sank at Libya's Benghazi naval base the following day, with Finnish defence officials and Libyan investigators attributing both attacks to Ukrainian forces [3][5][10]. The strikes opened two maritime theaters far from the front lines as Russia simultaneously attacked 18 Ukrainian locations with 186 drones overnight, killing at least 14 people in the Kyiv region [21][29].

Two Ukrainian drones struck the cargo vessel Sparta II in international waters west of Gogland island in the eastern Gulf of Finland, starting a fire that forced the crew to evacuate into lifeboats [17][22]. The damaged ship drifted into Finnish territorial waters, prompting a two-day multinational firefighting and towing operation before it was hauled back into Russian waters [3][31]. Finland's Defence Ministry said the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine, an assessment echoed by Finnish Defence Forces representative Aki-Mauri Huhtinen at a press conference [18][42]. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo said the war is spilling into Finland's immediate vicinity and that Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor [3][22]. Estonian Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna called the incident «побічним ефектом російської загарбницької війни та самооборони України» (a side effect of Russia's aggressive war and Ukraine's self-defense), and Estonian Prime Minister Kristen Michal said the consequences of the fight against Russian aggression have reached the Baltic Sea [26][34].

Russia's ambassador to Libya, Haidar Aganin, accused Kyiv of using «حملات تروریستی» (terrorist attacks) to open new fronts in the Mediterranean and Baltic and draw third parties into the conflict [7]. President Vladimir Putin vowed a "much more painful" response to Ukrainian strikes on the Crimea bridge, according to reporting that placed his remarks after a summit with Trump and Xi [29]. Russia's Defence Ministry said air defenses destroyed 170 Ukrainian fixed-wing drones overnight over seven Russian regions, annexed Crimea, and the Black and Azov seas, and Russian authorities reported no damage to critical infrastructure [13].

In Libya, satellite imagery from the firm Vantor identified the sunken vessel at Benghazi as the Russian Navy corvette Merkury, a Steregushchiy-class warship, with most of the vessel capsized and only the bow visible [5][8][12]. A second sea-drone attack later struck a Maltese-flagged commercial vessel offshore, with all crew rescued [24][28]. Libya's eastern-based administration condemned the strikes as a violation of sovereignty, stating: "The Libyan government will not allow Libyan ports, territory or territorial waters to be turned into an arena for settling scores or exporting international conflicts" [10]. The Tripoli-based Government of National Unity warned that hosting illegal foreign forces could import external conflicts into Libya [6][33]. The UN mission in Libya called for "an immediate, independent and transparent investigation" and said Libya must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts [28][33]. Eastern Prime Minister Osama Hammad sent official requests to the UN General Assembly and Security Council and demanded Ukraine explain its experts' activities in Libya [24].

Naval analyst HI Sutton said the attack was probably conducted by sea drones, and Jalel Harchaoui of the Royal United Services Institute said Ukraine is "the only actor brazen enough to pull something like that off" [10][28]. Eastern Libyan officials said initial investigation indicated multiple maritime drones were launched from a Ukrainian-operated base in western Libya [24]. Prothom Alo reported that a small Ukrainian special forces team stationed in Misrata is believed to have coordinated drone attacks on Russian ships over the past year [19].

Reporting grounded in Ukrainian intelligence and sanctions documentation identified the Sparta II as belonging to Oboronlogistika, the Russian Defence Ministry's sole official carrier, and linked the vessel to past arms shipments including Russian military equipment from Syria [14][17]. Finnish Border Guard representative Ilya Ilin said it cannot be ruled out that military materiel was on board [30]. Russia's Tass, citing an unnamed source, said the vessel carried paints and coatings [18].

Lithuania's Foreign Minister Kestutis Budrys stated that Lithuania has never authorized the use of its airspace for attacks against targets in Russia [15]. Lithuania's National Crisis Management Centre said images circulating on social media were inauthentic and warned that such messages may be intended to create the false impression that Lithuania or NATO is facilitating attacks on Russian targets [34].

On the Ukrainian side of the exchange, the Air Force reported that Russia attacked with 186 strike drones, including 72 jet-powered Shaheds, and that air defenses downed or suppressed 161 drones while hits were recorded at 18 locations [21]. A five-story apartment building collapsed in Dymer, Kyiv region, killing at least 10 people, and a drone strike on a NOVUS supermarket in Vyshneve killed three [16][29][48]. Ukrainian human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets called the attacks part of Russia's genocidal war and urged the world to "stop the killing of innocent Ukrainians" [29]. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia launched approximately 2,700 drones, 1,460 aerial bombs, and 165 missiles at 20 Ukrainian regions in one week and urged the US to authorize Patriot missile transfers under PURL [25]. Amnesty International warned that Russia's increased attacks on energy and heating infrastructure are "creating a deadly situation for civilians" [50].

Russian-appointed officials reported that a Ukrainian strike on Russian-controlled Luhansk killed six people, including a 13-year-old, and damaged homes and infrastructure [1].

Russia began towing the Sparta II back into Russian waters on November 23, with thermal cameras showing some containers still warm but the vessel stable [4][31]. Estonia's navy commander Ivo Vark said the risk of further drone attacks in the Gulf of Finland cannot be ruled out [4]. Libya's eastern administration announced a joint investigation with Moscow, and the UN mission's call for an independent probe remains outstanding [12][28].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
