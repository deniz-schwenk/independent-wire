Russia Launches 172-Drone Barrage on Kyiv as Ukraine Strikes Russian Refineries

Overnight exchange damages Kyiv's energy grid and Northern Bridge days before trilateral talks resume in Abu Dhabi.

Russia launched 172 drones and 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv overnight on 6–7 November, damaging the Northern Bridge, energy infrastructure across 12 regions, and substations connecting the Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear plants to the capital, while Ukrainian forces struck a Rosneft refinery in Saratov and a military-components plant in Oryol in a reciprocal overnight exchange [2][4][5][7][11][27]. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia fired 36 ballistic missiles at Kyiv and its region, of which Ukrainian air defenses intercepted four, and accused Moscow of trying to "plunge Kyiv and the entire country into a blackout" ahead of a fourth round of trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi [9][7]. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko described the capital's energy supply situation as "extremely difficult," with disruptions to heating and water systems, and said the Northern Bridge was closed in both directions [4][28]. Zelensky said nine people were injured nationwide, including a child; one man was killed in the Brovary district of Kyiv Oblast, and Kyiv Independent's tally over the past day put the nationwide toll at two killed and 37 injured [4][25][36]. A Kyiv resident named Iryna told RFI that residents are exhausted by the constant lack of electricity and heat, to the point that even improvements go unnoticed [15].

Russia's Ministry of Defence said its forces carried out a "massive" strike on military-industrial, energy and transport infrastructure in and around Kyiv, including the Northern Bridge — which it said was used to transport military equipment — and the Meridian plant, which it said produces Flamingo cruise missiles [4][7][24]. Russian Ambassador to the UN Vassily Nebenzia told the Security Council that Kyiv had launched a 40-day operation against Russian refineries, fuel depots and airfields, and that Russian strikes were restricted to military-industrial and supporting energy facilities [39]. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov described Ukraine's strike on an Iranian-flagged vessel in the Caspian Sea as an attack on Iran and said the Kyiv threat must be neutralised [50].

Ukraine's General Staff reported that its forces struck two Iskander missile launchers and a Pantsir-S1 air defense system in Bryansk Oblast [12]. Ukrainian drones set the Saratov Rosneft refinery on fire and caused a fire at the Proton-Elektroteks plant in Oryol, which produces components for military equipment, though the General Staff said rocket forces struck the Oryol plant with Neptune missiles [27][31][38]. Ukrainian Navy spokesperson Dmytro Pletenchuk said Russia faces a shortage of air defense systems, with some military airfields having no cover at all or only one or two Pantsir systems [12]. Saratov Governor Roman Busargin reported damage to civilian infrastructure and preliminary casualties, while Oryol Governor Andrey Klychkov confirmed damage to factory facilities but no injuries [27][11].

UN Assistant Secretary-General Khaled Khiari told the Security Council that Russian attacks had reached "a new horrifying level," with over 100 civilians killed and 429 injured in a week, while also reporting 496 civilians killed and 3,394 injured in Russia from Ukrainian attacks between January and September [39]. Council of Europe Commissioner for Human Rights Michael O'Flaherty condemned a Russian attack on a school building in Komyshuvakha where civilians had gathered for humanitarian assistance, said double-tap strikes on civilian targets amount to war crimes, and warned that strikes on energy infrastructure deprive civilians of heat, water and shelter [40]. Ukraine's UN Ambassador Andrii Melnyk said Russia's "missile terror" had reached an unprecedented level of brutality and that Moscow's objective was to make Kyiv unliveable [39]. Kenyan Ambassador Martin Kimani said early in the war that Ukraine's territorial integrity is being violated and that the UN Charter is wilting under the invasion of the powerful [49].

Ukrainian officials stressed that air defenses intercepted only four of 36 ballistic missiles, and Zelensky said Ukraine has been waiting nearly a month for US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to approve Patriot interceptor deliveries that European allies have already funded [13][37]. Finnish Defence Minister Antti Häkkänen said the US is "playing politics" with Patriot missiles, with interceptors paid for by European allies sitting awaiting American approval [37]. Ukrainian military intelligence estimates Russia produces more than 200 cruise and ballistic missiles monthly, and presidential adviser Serhii Beskrestnov said Russia is integrating AI into drones that can navigate without GPS, producing up to 3,000 Shahed and 700 Geranium-5 drones per month [12][3].

US Deputy Permanent Representative Jeffrey Bartos urged Kyiv and Moscow to return to the negotiating table, saying the war cannot end through military victory on either side [39]. President Donald Trump said a "big beautiful agreement" involving "all of Europe" could be close, and Jared Kushner said discussions would include a new security framework for Europe alongside guarantees for Ukraine [7][9]. Zelensky called Kushner's blueprint "Yalta 2.0" and warned against Ukraine being divided by Russia [9]. German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius said recent months offer little confidence that Trump's approach will quickly produce a ceasefire [17]. Turkey's Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan called for continuation of the Istanbul talks and proposed a moratorium sparing energy infrastructure and shipping from attack [48]. China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said dialogue and negotiation are the only viable path and called on parties to de-escalate [46].

The energy war's effects extended beyond the battlefield. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent attributed elevated global energy prices to Ukraine's drone strikes on Russian energy infrastructure alongside Middle East tensions affecting Strait of Hormuz shipping [43]. The International Energy Agency reported EU gas storage entering winter at its lowest since 2013 [41]. BBC Uzbek reported that Kazakhstan cut output at Karachaganak after the Orenburg gas plant was hit, Kyrgyzstan faced petrol shortages, and Uzbekistan Airways reduced routes over jet-fuel scarcity [42]. Indian media warned that Ukrainian strikes on Russia's shadow tanker fleet could cut discounted crude making up more than 20 percent of India's imports [44].

South Korea's National Security Office Director Wi Sung-lac has said Russia-North Korea military cooperation violates UN Security Council resolutions and is a direct concern for South Korean security; Zelensky said the barrage's missiles included North Korean KN-23s [45][2]. Leaked Rosstat data published by the hacking groups Silent Crow and Belarus Cyber Partisans put Russian deaths in the war at more than 450,000 by Silent Crow's count and 393,881 by Belarus Cyber Partisans' narrower methodology, making it Russia's deadliest conflict since World War II, with British Defence Ministry assessments putting deaths above half a million and casualties potentially as high as 1.5 million including wounded [33].

Within Kyiv, Zelensky publicly blamed Klitschko for the capital's power outages, calling them consequences of unimplemented municipal decisions on heating-season preparation [8][17]. Klitschko rejected the accusations, said city representatives were not invited to relevant consultations, and asked how the capital could have prevented Russia's operation against Ukraine's energy sector [8][17].

Trilateral talks between Russia, Ukraine and the United States are scheduled to resume in Abu Dhabi, with Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky heading Russia's delegation [6]. UAE Deputy Prime Minister Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed Al Nahyan welcomed the hosting of the talks and credited Trump's facilitation [47].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
