The USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in the US Central Command area of responsibility on November 15, bringing the number of American carrier strike groups in the region to three for the first time since April [4][7][23]. The deployment came as CNN, citing seven US officials, reported that US intelligence indicates Iran is developing plans to attack senior Trump administration officials in Washington with a radiological "dirty bomb" or chemical weapon, though officials described the intelligence as options planning rather than confirmation of an imminent attack [16][20][33]. The White House said it is monitoring all potential threats but that there are currently no credible threats against US territory [16][20]. Iran's mission to the United Nations rejected the chemical-weapons accusations as "false and fabricated" without directly addressing the reported dirty-bomb planning [16][33].

Attorney General Todd Blanche, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, and CIA Director John Ratcliffe sent a joint letter to Congress urging renewal of a lapsed foreign surveillance law, warning that delay endangers Americans at home and service members abroad [16][20]. The US Embassy in Bahrain urged American citizens to exercise heightened vigilance over possible attacks by Iranian-supported Houthis and Iranian efforts to target US officials and facilities [13][28]. Israel Hayom reported that US intelligence believes radiological material may already have been smuggled into the United States [33], and the Secret Service relocated Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to a military compound following Iranian threats [33].

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it launched six drones at a US air-defense radar at the Al-Rifaa helicopter base in Bahrain on November 15, describing the strike as retaliation for "hostile US actions" [7][9][15]. Bahrain's army said its air defenses intercepted and destroyed the projectiles, accusing Tehran of a "systematic hostile attitude" toward the kingdom [7][15]. Bahrain's Foreign Ministry called the attack "heinous aggression" and a flagrant violation of international law [9][19]. Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Jordan, and Egypt condemned the strikes; Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry called them a violation of international law and good-neighbourliness [19]. Bahraini resident Hayat Ibrahim said she had hoped Iranian attacks would not resume, saying they bring "worry and uncertainty" [9][19].

Iranian officials framed the Bahrain strikes and the Strait of Hormuz disruptions as retaliation for a US naval blockade and joint US-Israeli attacks on Iran [4][25][34]. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Iran will not wait for a US first strike, stating: "Iran is not Cuba that you can blockade, nor is it Venezuela that you can force to surrender" [2][11]. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said Iran is "not waiting for anyone" [9][19]. Iran's Revolutionary Guard naval arm said six tankers have burned or sunk in the Strait of Hormuz since Sunday after hitting mines, and warned that vessels cooperating with US officers "will become prey to fire" [34][35]. US Central Command has said it recently cleared all mines from the strait's main routes [19]. Iran's army said it shot down a US MQ-1 drone over the strait on Monday [21]. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Organization reported a tanker was struck by an "unknown projectile" while exiting the strait, causing an engine-room fire [26][35]. Lloyd's List assessed that strait traffic remains below a third of prewar levels [26]. The Council of the European Union listed the IRGC navy's Hormozgan command and two individuals in June, arguing Iran's toll-and-filter system violates international transit-passage rights [37].

President Donald Trump said the war is "militarily effectively over" while warning that US forces remain "locked and loaded" and that he would not hesitate to act if "something does not work out" [2][26]. Trump shared a message saying "May God protect our troops" as the Roosevelt arrived [24]. He said he no longer needs a deal with Iran and instructed Vice President JD Vance to step away from negotiations [1][2]. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the economic pressure campaign is "so devastating that right now there is no need for major combat operations" [2][29]. Vance publicly broke with Trump, saying "the reason we lost is the war" [26].

Mediating states pressed both sides toward diplomacy. Qatar's Prime Minister Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani said a perfect peace agreement will never be reached but that an acceptable understanding to reduce tensions is needed [6][22]. Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan and Al Thani discussed de-escalation efforts by phone [27]. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told an Economic Cooperation Organization meeting in Islamabad that nine regional countries support Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days in exchange for unfreezing assets, lifting sanctions, and ending the naval blockade [10][25]. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said Moscow hopes Washington is no longer considering military scenarios and praised Qatari, Pakistani, and Egyptian mediation [32]. The Russian Foreign Ministry described the crisis as resulting from "unprovoked aggression by the United States and Israel against Iran" [31]. Gharibabadi said Washington may exploit diplomacy to buy time for a new attack and has violated its commitments at least three times in 18 months [3].

Ali Vaez of the International Crisis Group said efforts to deter another war "could inadvertently ignite one that neither can win, contain or easily end" [2]. Rashid al-Mohanadi of the Center for International Policy Research said divergent perceptions of leverage make diplomacy difficult, with Iranians perceiving they have more leverage and Americans perceiving they have more [2]. Israeli opposition figures Gadi Eisenkot and Avigdor Lieberman warned that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu might exploit the transitional government period to resume war with Iran and pressure them into a unity government [18].

War-risk premiums for vessels transiting the Gulf have risen to 7.5–10 percent of hull value, according to Sinopec News [38]. Lloyd's insurer Beazley set up a US$1 billion marine war consortium at Lloyd's split between hull war and cargo war [30]. Wisit Limluecha, vice chairman of the Thai Chamber of Commerce, reported premiums of US$2,000–4,000 per container versus US$300–1,000 normally, with some carriers suspending Middle East bookings [42]. South Korea convened an emergency meeting between deputy trade minister Moon Shin-hak and the CEOs of four major refiners after a drone attack halted Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline [39]. Pakistan Stock Exchange director Ahmad Chinoy warned that a Hormuz closure could push crude to US$110–120 [43]. Kenya's Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority froze pump prices, drawing KSh 4.6 billion from a petroleum levy to absorb import-cost increases [41]. Inside Iran, Euronews Persian reported inflation near 90 percent and the dollar above 260,000 toman, with economist Masoud Nili describing the economy as sitting between chronic inflation and hyperinflation [36]. Brazil's Petrobras posted quarterly earnings of R$39.75 billion on a near-US$97 barrel, with president Magda Chambriard saying record diesel output was "essential to guaranteeing Brazil's energy security during this geopolitical crisis" [40].

In the US Congress, Representative Thomas Massie introduced a War Powers resolution on November 10 directing the president to remove US forces from hostilities with Iran [29]. Representative Jason Crow said "we are now in week 37 of Trump's '2 to 3 week' war with Iran" [29]. Representative Tom Cole argued that a Republican Congress will not send a Republican president bills he would veto [29]. A Reuters/Ipsos poll showed 71 percent of respondents want Congress to end the conflict [29].

The White House and Kremlin confirmed a first-ever trilateral summit between Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin, and Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on Thursday, focused on nuclear disarmament and Iran [5]. Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei issued a directive on October 31 ordering retaliation without geographic limits [29]. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, met Putin at the Kremlin in mid-November and handed over seven Iranian conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz [31].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
