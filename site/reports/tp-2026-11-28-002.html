Zelenskyy Warns Russia Is Preparing New Large-Scale Attack as Ukraine Strikes 11 Russian Regions

Ukrainian president says intelligence shows Russia finalizing a mass strike on energy and infrastructure; Kyiv reports retaliatory strikes on oil and military facilities across Russian territory and the Black Sea.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that Russia is in the final stages of preparing a new large-scale attack on Ukraine's energy, logistics, and basic-service infrastructure, citing Ukrainian intelligence [26][25]. "Unfortunately, we have information that the Russians are preparing a new large-scale attack," Zelenskyy said, urging attention to missile alerts and vowing Ukraine would rebuild and respond to every strike [26]. He described conditions as especially difficult in Dnipro, Pavlohrad, Kharkiv, Odesa, Sumy, Donetsk, and Mykolaiv regions [26]. The warning came as Russian overnight ballistic missile strikes on Kyiv wounded seven people and damaged a shopping centre and two other buildings, according to Mayor Vitali Klitschko [2].

Russian strikes across northern Ukraine killed at least six people and wounded 23, hitting civilian enterprises and water infrastructure in Chernihiv and Zhytomyr regions [31]. In Nizhyn, a strike on the water supply killed two and wounded four, according to Mayor Oleksandr Kodola, who warned more victims may be under the rubble, while Ukrainian authorities separately reported three killed and seven wounded in a missile strike on an enterprise in the town [31][13]. In Kharkiv, Russian drones attacked the Shevchenkivskyi and Kyivskyi districts, injuring three, while overnight strikes on Izium injured six [22]. The UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs told the Security Council that repeated attacks are threatening Ukraine's winter preparedness [41]. Russia denies deliberately targeting civilian infrastructure [22].

The Russian Ministry of Defense stated that its overnight strikes hit military-industrial facilities, including the Thermoplast plant producing attack drones in Khmelnytskyi, an electrical equipment plant in Zaporizhzhia, a solid rocket fuel logistics center near Kyiv, and a military cargo ship in the Black Sea southwest of Odesa [8]. The ministry said strikes near Dnipropetrovsk damaged three railway locomotives used for military shipments — a figure another ministry statement put at four — and that a dry cargo ship carrying dual-use goods for Ukrainian forces was hit in the Black Sea southwest of Odesa [3][8]. Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said Russian air defenses destroyed 11 drones flying toward Moscow [13]. Vladimir Putin warned of inevitable retaliatory measures against attacks on refineries and proposed discussing additional protection for the plants [44].

Zelenskyy said Ukrainian forces struck military and oil facilities in 11 Russian regions and the Black Sea over the past week, listing targets including a Transneft facility in Bashkortostan, a Krasnodar refinery, and the Marinovka airfield [15]. "Ukraine responds to every Russian strike absolutely justly," he said [15]. Reuters data cited by the Kyiv Independent indicates the attacks disrupted 15 of Russia's 35 major refineries and cut crude processing from 700,000 to around 400,000 metric tons per day [15]. Russia banned aviation kerosene exports until spring amid fuel shortages [32]. A Ukrainian-made Blysk laser system shot down a Russian Geran jet-powered drone at roughly 800 metres, described by presidential defence adviser Serhii Beskrestnov as a world first [21].

Ukraine's naval drone campaign has reached the Mediterranean, where forces attacked the Russian corvette Merkury and the cargo ship MV Angara near Libya on November 22, sinking the corvette and damaging the cargo vessel [39]. A Russian convoy carrying military equipment to Libya turned around in the Mediterranean after the strike [39]. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni said the attack risks spreading the war to the Mediterranean [5][36]. Zelenskyy confirmed to Meloni that the operation was carried out without prior coordination with European partners, after Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani publicly rejected Zelenskyy's earlier coordination assertion [33]. Libyan Presidential Council deputy head Musa al-Koni called the attack a de facto declaration of war [27], while security expert Elisabeth Braw said Ukraine is showing it can hit Russian vessels wherever they operate and University of Bonn's Joachim Weber warned that the likelihood of an accidental exchange of fire is rising [7].

Donald Trump posted a photo of himself with Putin at the APEC summit in Shenzhen captioned "`mi amigo` (my friend)" and said he urged Putin to pull back military aggression across Europe and wants to make a deal [9][2][5]. Zelenskyy called the friendship remark strange, comparing it to calling Hitler a friend during World War II, said Putin is "an aggressor, and a killer" who cannot be a friend, and complained that delayed Patriot deliveries and Starlink restrictions are costing Ukrainian lives [5][9]. Trump's envoy Jared Kushner called Putin a "grandmaster of politics" who showed restraint by not using nuclear weapons, drawing outrage in Kyiv and among European allies [19]. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Zelenskyy's remarks show he "is not willing to follow the path of (a) peace settlement" [6]. A Bangladeshi commentary cited EU foreign-policy chief Kaja Kallas saying more than 25,000 sanctions have cost Russia about $1.5 trillion without changing Putin's policy, and argued Ukraine should negotiate, cede territory, and abandon NATO hopes [20].

European governments and thinktanks are documenting what they describe as a Russian hybrid campaign across the continent. An International Institute for Strategic Studies assessment found Russian sabotage in Europe rose 16 percent in 12 months, with arson and explosive attacks roughly doubling [10][28]. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said two missiles and a jet-powered drone entered Polish airspace and convened an urgent meeting with the military and security services, while Canada summoned Russia's ambassador to protest the incursions [30][46]. Estonia announced it would close all border crossings with Russia from December 1 after what its internal security service said was a GRU-ordered arson attempt against a defence company [29]. Russia rejects all Western accusations of sabotage [28][29].

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha proposed a Black Sea truce with international partners to secure food exports, thanking India, Turkey, and Egypt for their mediation roles [32]. China's Foreign Ministry stated that dialogue and negotiation are the only viable way to resolve the Ukraine crisis [51]. Russian senators introduced bills banning unauthorized publication of information about the consequences of drone, missile, and sabotage attacks, with fines up to 50,000 rubles and prison terms up to five years; lawyer Evgeny Smirnov said the bills exclude expert assessment of whether information is false, making cases easy to investigate [18][34]. Belarusian President Alexander Lukashenko questioned Putin's historical claim to Ukrainian land, saying that if history justified territorial claims, Russia should be returned to Mongolia [1]. Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan plans December talks in Moscow on the Russian military base and has questioned its necessity [4], while Ukrainian intelligence said North Korea has temporarily halted some military cooperation with Russia, including missile and artillery shell deliveries [2][48].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
