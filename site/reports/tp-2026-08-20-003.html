Ukraine Launches Over 600 Drones at Moscow as Russia Deploys Jet-Powered Drones Across Ukrainian Cities

Civilian casualties mount on both sides as Kyiv and Moscow trade accusations of deliberate targeting, while NATO neighbors scramble aircraft and the UN reports the highest monthly civilian death toll in Ukraine since 2022.

Ukraine launched more than 600 drones toward the Moscow region overnight in what Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin described as the largest such attack in two years, with 180 of 620 UAVs destroyed according to Moscow officials [3][4], while Novaya Gazeta reported a figure of 637 drones [12]. Russia's Defense Ministry reported intercepting 453 Ukrainian drones across Russia and annexed Crimea [11]. The barrage injured three people in the Moscow region, including a 10-year-old girl, and damaged residential buildings, a Wildberries warehouse, and a transformer substation that left over 5,000 subscribers without electricity [12]. Ukrainian drones also struck an oil refinery in Bashkortostan, over 1,000 km from the Ukrainian border, injuring one person, according to regional head Radiy Khabirov [11]. International wire coverage presented the Moscow drone barrage and Russian strikes on Ukrainian cities side by side as mutual large-scale escalation [31][32][39].

Russian forces carried out strikes across Ukrainian cities using Shahed-type drones, Iskander-M ballistic missiles, and jet-powered Banderol munitions [8][25]. A Russian missile strike on Pechenihy village in Kharkiv region killed 10 people and wounded 18, hitting a busy intersection with a post office and shops surrounded by residential buildings [4][9]. In Kherson, a Russian drone struck a civilian minibus, killing four elderly passengers and wounding at least four others, with some accounts reporting five wounded [1][8][20][21]. A separate drone attack on a central market in Zaporizhzhia destroyed a shopping complex and killed one person [17]. Russian forces also struck the regional police headquarters in Zhytomyr with Banderol jet-powered drones, killing two officers and injuring at least 40 people [24]. A heavy Russian bombardment of Kyiv killed at least nine people across the city and surrounding region [6], though other outlets reported tolls ranging from five to six killed [15][19]. Strikes hit twelve locations across three districts, damaging residential buildings, a medical facility, and an educational institution [6][15].

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy described the Kherson minibus attack as a brutal "drone safari" that had crossed all boundaries of what a normal person could imagine, and said it was absolutely clear the bus was civilian [5][8]. The Kherson military-civilian administration said the city had become a place where Russian drones continuously hunt civilians through systematic attacks on cars, buses, cyclists, and pedestrians [18]. Russian forces also attacked an emergency medical team with a drone in Kherson's Korabelnyi district after the team arrived in response to a call, injuring two women aged 70 and 45 [23]. Ukraine's Prosecutor General's Office and the Zaporizhzhia Regional Prosecutor's Office launched war-crime investigations under Article 438 of Ukraine's Criminal Code [17][18].

Moscow has repeatedly affirmed that it only targets Ukrainian military infrastructure and minimizes civilian casualties [18]. Russian officials, in turn, accused Ukraine of deliberately targeting civilians in deep strikes inside Russian territory. Krasnodar Krai Governor Veniamin Kondratyev accused Ukraine of intentionally attacking civilians after a drone strike on a crowded beach killed seven people including three children [38]. A Ukrainian drone strike on Tatarstan killed 13 people including seven Uzbek nationals [37]. In Belgorod region, a Ukrainian drone struck a vehicle, killing a woman and hospitalizing a male passenger [22]. Andriy Kovalenko, head of the Ukrainian Center for Countering Disinformation, stated that Russian civilian deaths resulting from the war should be blamed on Putin's decision to wage it, rather than on Ukraine's strike choices [37].

The exchange introduced new weapons technology on both sides. The Ukrainian Air Force reported that Russia launched 76 jet-powered Shahed-type drones on August 18, with sometimes two-thirds of all daily drones being jet-powered [10]. Yurii Ihnat, head of the Air Force communications department, said Russia launched five times as many jet-powered drones in July as in June, with their flight parameters approaching those of cruise missiles [10][28]. Satellite analysis identified up to 10 new Russian drone launch sites near NATO's eastern flank, with longer rails for jet-powered Geran-4 and Geran-5 systems that could reach parts of Poland and the Baltics [30]. On the Ukrainian side, geopolitical analyst Ulrich Bounat noted that Ukraine now sends 500-600 drones daily toward Moscow, with the FP-1 drone produced at 300 units per day, and is developing the FP-9 ballistic missile carrying 800 kg of explosives [14].

Zelenskyy linked a Ukrainian strike on a Moscow-region oil refinery to a prior Russian attack on the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra, framing Ukraine's long-range campaign as reciprocal retaliation [33]. He reported that Russia launched over 1,550 attack drones, nearly 1,560 guided aerial bombs, and 62 missiles in the past week [39]. Zelenskyy criticized allies for hesitating to provide additional protective equipment while Russia acts without restraint [5][18]. Security and defense expert Nico Lange argued that Ukraine needs deep-strike weapons like Taurus plus harder sanctions, including a comprehensive Schengen visa ban for Russians and blocking the shadow fleet, rather than what he called naive calls for negotiation [13].

Russia's embassy in London warned that reported British-made drones used in strikes on Russian territory "confirm that London is deliberately opting for an escalation of the Ukraine crisis, while hypocritically professing a desire for peace" [4]. The embassy stated that "London's actions will inevitably carry consequences for which it will have to answer" [3]. A British defence ministry spokesperson responded that Britain stands shoulder to shoulder with Ukraine and is committed to providing the equipment Ukraine needs to defend itself against Russia [3].

The UN reported that civilian deaths in Ukraine reached 437 in July, the highest monthly toll since May 2022 [2][18]. UN Secretary-General António Guterres and humanitarian coordinator Tom Fletcher stressed that states must protect aid workers and uphold international humanitarian law [29]. MSF Country Director Robin Meldrum stated that a drone strike on a passenger bus in Kherson is a violation of International Humanitarian Law and that civilians must never be the target [40].

Neighboring states took defensive precautions as the drone and missile exchange extended beyond the combat zone. Poland's military command launched preventive military air operations to protect Polish airspace, and Romania's defence ministry reported that a drone entered Romanian airspace before crashing in an unpopulated area of Tulcea county [6][19]. New Zealand's Civil Aviation Authority issued a NOTAM warning after Russia's State Air Traffic Management Corporation informed it of a planned missile launch potentially affecting international airspace [7].

Ukraine's strikes on Russian infrastructure have also produced third-country economic effects. Since July 18, Ukrainian strikes have hit at least 20 Wildberries facilities, damaging over 1.18 million square meters of warehouse capacity [16]. Kyrgyz e-commerce retailers who use the platform as a vital export channel reported destroyed inventories and bankruptcy risk, with one entrepreneur losing approximately $42,000, and Kyrgyz authorities announced preferential loans and tax holidays for affected garment producers [16].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.

Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.

Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
