Russia Strikes Kyiv Bridges and Warns Foreigners to Leave as Zelenskyy Vows Refinery Campaign

Moscow frames the bridge strikes as retaliation on military logistics; Kyiv calls them a campaign to empty Ukrainian cities, as Moldova reports airspace violations and diplomatic mediation efforts advance.

Russia's Foreign Ministry urged foreign nationals and diplomats to leave Kyiv, warning that those who remain are "knowingly exposing themselves to mortal danger" and that Russian forces will continue "massive retaliatory strikes" on the capital [1][6][22][25][32]. The warning followed drone strikes on Kyiv's Pivnichnyi (Northern) Bridge — the second major Dnipro crossing hit in two days — which injured two people and blocked traffic between the city's left and right banks [3][5][21][24]. Russia's Defense Ministry said the bridge "facilitates troop movements and the logistics of military cargo deliveries" for Ukraine's armed forces [8][20][29].

Four Kyiv bridges have been struck since Thursday, severing road, rail and trolleybus links across the Dnipro for the capital's roughly three million residents [7][18][28][29]. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the capital is in a "very dramatic situation" and that "the enemy is tearing Kyiv apart" [9][12][28]. Infrastructure Minister Mykola Kalashnyk said the government is preparing pontoon bridges and planning a tunnel under the riverbed to prevent the city from being cut off [7]. Aviation expert Kostiantyn Kryvolap said destroying the bridges with 50-kilogram drone warheads is "practically impossible" and that the strikes function as "mosquito bites" intended to halt traffic and exert psychological pressure [14][24].

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Ukrainian intelligence obtained documents showing Putin issued a "new doctrine" authorizing strikes on roads, schools, hospitals and infrastructure to pressure people to leave cities [2][6][15][31]. "They want Ukrainians to leave the capital... and show that if you don't have the capital you don't have the country," Zelenskyy said [9]. Putin said Russia's strikes are a direct response to Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries and Black Sea ships, adding: "Now from every direction we're receiving calls 'let's stop'. But they started it" [5][10]. Putin said Ukraine's refinery strikes have cost Russia about one percent of GDP [12].

Zelenskyy vowed to step up attacks on Russian oil refineries in response, saying: "With their attacks on our energy, we have to respond on their energy. But we will not respond, of course, just like them, on any civilian objects" [2][23][31][47]. Ukraine's military said its drones targeted the Lukoil refinery in Volgograd and another facility in Samara region [12]. Ukrainian missile manufacturer Fire Point said its longer-range FP-9 missile could reach Moscow, and the FP-7 ballistic missile was used in combat for the first time [23].

U.S. President Donald Trump urged Ukraine to stop striking Russian diesel refineries, saying the attacks are driving up global fuel prices [15][17][23][37]. Market analysts countered that the diesel price surge is driven primarily by the U.S.-Iran war and Gulf disruptions. Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG Group, said Ukrainian strikes are "only a small ingredient" in the price surge [37]. Andreas Krieg, a senior lecturer at King's College London, said "the Iran war remains the larger systemic shock to global diesel availability" [37]. Brazil's Petrobras said it is studying plans to reach diesel self-sufficiency after the Gulf conflict disrupted supplies [45].

The war extended beyond Ukraine's borders as Moldova reported five Russian aerial weapons — including Banderol missiles and drones — violated its airspace and exploded on its soil [6][20][22][48]. President Maia Sandu said "its war is dangerously reaching Moldova" and Moldova summoned Russia's ambassador with a protest note [5][48]. NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte called for calm and continued support for Ukraine, saying Russia is testing NATO through airspace violations, sabotage and hybrid attacks [47]. Ukrainian military intelligence warned that Russia's secret services have been tasked with increasing hybrid operations in EU and NATO states [14]. Lithuania temporarily closed Vilnius airport after an unidentified aerial object was detected, with NATO jets deployed; Lithuanian officials said it was not certain the object was a drone [11].

Russian and Moscow-installed officials reported Ukrainian strikes on Russian and occupied territories. Kaluga Governor Vladislav Shapsha said Ukrainian drones damaged residential buildings and injured several people [4][42]. Belgorod regional governor Alexander Shuvayev said a Ukrainian drone attack injured six people including three children [43]. Moscow-installed Luhansk head Leonid Pasechkin reported two people killed in Ukrainian drone attacks [8][20], and Russian-installed authorities in Zaporizhzhia reported one killed and 10 injured [2]. A Ukrainian drone strike on Tatarstan killed eight Uzbek migrant workers, prompting analysts to criticize Uzbekistan's silence and call for Kyiv to apologize and compensate the families [49].

Residents of Kyiv described night evacuations, metro-station sheltering and commuting chaos. Tetiana Semyhailo said she was woken by an explosion at 6:18 a.m. that injured her husband and forced them to flee their burning apartment [7]. Nadia Andrianova said nights in Kyiv became very dangerous, prompting her to relocate with her daughter to Uzhgorod near the Slovak border [7]. A 64-year-old taxi driver said: "Tout est bloqué" (Everything is blocked), describing the paralysis after the South Bridge closure [28]. Valeriya Tkachenko, a lawyer, said: "I'm scared to death all day about what could happen to my son" [9]. Roman Samchuk said he and his wife would not leave, citing "Una buena puerta española" (A good Spanish door) as sufficient protection [7].

A Russian strike on a Liberian-flagged cargo ship in the Odesa region killed one sailor and injured three, with 13 crew evacuated according to Ukrainian authorities, though Al Jazeera reported 12 [2][4][36]. Russia's Defense Ministry said the vessel carried dual-use goods [4]. Ukraine's Sea Ports Authority called it "yet another deliberate attack by Russia on civilian shipping" [36]. Ukraine's Energy Minister Denys Shmyhal briefed European partners on an intercepted Russian plan to use up to 1,000 missiles, drones and bombs to collapse Ukraine's energy system this winter, targeting power substations, hydro and thermal plants, and substations serving three nuclear plants [9][35]. Former nuclear regulator Hryhorii Plachkov warned that strikes on high-voltage lines could sever three Ukrainian nuclear plants from the grid, splitting the energy system into isolated "islands" [46].

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said Kyiv has received four ceasefire proposals and identified India's as the most comprehensive, signaling readiness for a package deal on energy infrastructure and a Black Sea ceasefire [10][38]. Kremlin spokesman Dmitriy Peskov said a Black Sea ceasefire is impossible because Ukraine is intransigent, but expressed readiness to discuss security with Turkey, India or Egypt [10]. China's UN ambassador Fu Cong called for restraint and respect for international humanitarian law [40], and Egypt's foreign ministry expressed appreciation for diplomatic efforts to convene direct talks [41]. Putin praised Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi's mediation [38].

Russia's draft 2027 budget raises defense spending to 17.1 trillion rubles ($205.2 billion), a post-Soviet record, funded by cuts to healthcare, education and social programs and a third consecutive year of tax increases [34]. Economist Boris Grozovsky said "everyone will pay for the war, as much as they can" [34]. Analyst Alexandra Prokopenko warned the government may soon reach the limits of revenue it can raise through taxes to fund the war [34]. Military analyst Mykola Bielieskov said Ukraine's battlefield gains are being undermined by aerial vulnerability: "You can be quite successful in the land domain, but if you are vulnerable in the air, especially in the rear, that is the vulnerability your opponent is going to exploit" [9]. Zelenskyy said Ukraine aims to produce up to 400 interceptor drones by the end of October, and Foreign Minister Sybiha said Ukraine needs at least 300 Patriot interceptor missiles for winter protection [12][14].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
