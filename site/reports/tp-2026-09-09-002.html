Twelve Countries Restrict Israeli Settlement Trade as Israel Closes UK Consulate in Jerusalem

The coordinated measures cite the ICJ's 2024 advisory opinion and settler violence; Israel retaliates with diplomatic and entry bans against Britain.

Twelve governments — the United Kingdom, France, Canada, Denmark, Spain, Finland, Ireland, Iceland, Norway, Poland, Portugal, and Sweden — confirmed their intention to impose national restrictions on trade in goods from Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank, with the UK announcing an import ban, a sanctions regime on companies and individuals facilitating settlement expansion, and a ban on arms exports that materially contribute to the occupation [4][8][19]. British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told parliament that the British government agrees ethnic cleansing of Palestinians is being perpetrated by settler terrorists and that the official UK position is that Israel's occupation is unlawful [30][31]. The joint declaration, announced by French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot, cited settler violence and settlement expansion, singled out Israel's E1 settlement tender for approximately 1,200 homes as unacceptable, and urged Israel to halt expansion and hold violent settlers accountable [4][17].

Israel retaliated by closing the British consulate in East Jerusalem, banning twelve British politicians including Jeremy Corbyn and Diane Abbott from entering Israel, removing British representatives from the US-led International Gaza Support Center, and ending UK training of Palestinian Authority security forces [7][10][33]. Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar called the British move "morally distorted" interference in a sovereign state's electoral process and described Miliband's accusations as "outrageous lies," warning that "if Britain acts against Israel, Israel will act against Britain" [7][8][19]. President Isaac Herzog called the sanctions "a grave miscalculation, a decision that will fall on the wrong side of history" and accused Britain of interfering in Israel's upcoming October elections [10][30]. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich demanded the expulsion of the British ambassador and denounced the British government as antisemitic, while National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir called for Israel to recognize Argentina's sovereignty over the Falkland Islands in retaliation [22][26][11].

The United States confirmed it will not follow the UK's lead. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Washington does not want destabilization in the West Bank and that "obviously, we're not going to do what the UK did" [7][19][30]. US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee called the sanctions "discrimination against the Jewish people" and warned that the US would "without a doubt" respond, predicting an enormous economic impact on British companies through possible action by individual US states as well as the federal level [2][10][30]. Huckabee has shared Israel's view that the West Bank was promised to the Jews by God, and President Donald Trump has shown no desire to curb Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's settlement plans [5].

The sanctioning governments and the UN human rights office grounded the measures in the International Court of Justice's July 2024 advisory opinion and a September 2024 UN General Assembly resolution calling for an end to imports from Israeli settlements [20][26]. UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Turk urged all states to assess whether they are entrenching Israel's unlawful presence and called on companies to end business practices supporting illegal settlements [20][29]. The French Foreign Ministry said the targeted restriction on settlement goods is compliant with international law and is not a boycott of Israel [17]. UK government minister Pat McFadden stressed the measures are not a boycott of all Israeli goods but an effort to preserve a viable Palestinian state alongside Israel [22].

Palestinian officials and civil-society groups welcomed the measures while insisting they are insufficient. The Palestinian Authority called the sanctions "an important practical step toward holding the settlement system accountable" [8][30], and Palestinian Ambassador to the UK Husam Zomlot described the decision as "a turning point in the UK-Palestine relationship" [5]. UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese called Miliband's statement a seismic shift but said the ban must be part of a paradigm shift toward implementing the ICJ advisory opinion [19][26]. Hamas spokesman Basem Naim called the bans correct but insufficient and urged Nuremberg-style prosecution of Israeli war criminals [32]. Oxfam called on France to extend the ban to all services and investments [44], and the British Green Party urged the government to follow the restrictions with a full arms embargo and wide-ranging sanctions [33].

British Jewish communal bodies expressed concern about the measures' impact. Chief Rabbi Ephraim Mirvis called the sanctions "truly a dark day for British Jews" and said they would "succeed only in bolstering the very extremism they seek to target" [8][30]. The Board of Deputies of British Jews expressed deep regret and warned the deteriorating UK-Israel relationship could damage security and economies and inflame community tensions [26][30]. Conservative shadow foreign secretary Tom Tugendhat warned that turning Israel into a pariah risks sectarian division and antisemitic violence on British streets [8][31]. The Jewish Leadership Council condemned the move as deeply irresponsible and said it places an increased target on British Jews [26][35].

Questions about the measures' practical impact were raised by officials and analysts on multiple sides. Israeli trade officials estimated settlement exports to Europe at approximately $250 million and to the UK at $45 million, calling the move more political than economic [40]. French Trade Minister Nicolas Forissier said he was not convinced a ban would have much impact because very little trade is involved [39]. RFI reported that settlement products are hidden inside Israeli exports through a fraudulent labelling system, citing a Global Echo study of nearly 6,000 agricultural shipments finding more than 17 percent contained settlement products [27]. Global Echo report author Jessica Stober argued that an import ban is more self-enforcing than labelling rules because it makes dealing in settlement goods commercially unviable [34]. Australian Foreign Minister Penny Wong cited concerns about implementation and unintended consequences in explaining Australia's decision not to join the blanket ban [24][36].

Settler leaders and Israeli ministers asserted Jewish rights to the land and vowed to continue expansion. Yesha Council president Israel Gantz condemned the trade restrictions as "an anti-semitic and shameful measure targeting Jews merely for living and working on their ancestral land" [17]. Smotrich openly defended settlement expansion as a way to bury the idea of a Palestinian state and announced plans for 1,000 new settlement homes [42][46]. Netanyahu vowed to continue establishing settlements despite international pressure [22]. Settler date farmer Avigdor Arbel called the boycotts disgusting but said the sector is ready to divert sales to other markets [34].

The measures have left Israel and several Western allies in a diplomatic standoff with no clear resolution. Germany, which did not sign the joint declaration, criticized Israel sharply [18]. Australia is preparing another round of targeted sanctions but will not join the blanket ban [24]. The EU remains divided on a collective ban, with EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas acknowledging not all member states are on board [39]. Ireland is progressing legislation on settlement goods but excluding services [38]. Saar warned that countermeasures could be taken against other countries acting against Israel [15].
