Israeli Forces Advance into Syrian Village, Strike Southern Lebanon, and Raid UNRWA Centre as Far-Right Lawmaker Demolishes West Bank Memorial

The simultaneous operations across three fronts draw sovereignty objections from Syria, the UN, and Lebanon, while Israel's government and opposition split over a lawmaker's unauthorized demolition.

Nearly all evaluative and emotionally charged language in the article is properly attributed to named sources. A few borderline cases exist: the article's own description of Ben-Gvir having 'celebrated' the UNRWA operation and the characterization of employees being 'expelled' from the facility involve word choices that carry slightly more editorial weight than strictly neutral alternatives, and the label 'far-right' for lawmaker Sukkot, while widely used, is applied without attribution.

Israeli forces advanced into the Syrian village of Jumla in the Yarmouk Basin of Daraa province, launched air strikes across southern Lebanon, and raided a UN-operated vocational training centre in occupied East Jerusalem, while far-right lawmaker Zvi Sukkot demolished a Palestinian memorial under military escort in the West Bank village of Madama [4][6][2]. The operations across multiple fronts occurred as Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani indicated US-mediated security talks with Israel are expected to resume [27].

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz stated that Israel will not withdraw from Mount Hermon or the security zone while jihadist threats persist, and Israeli Army Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir warned that hostile forces will not be allowed to establish a base on the border [15][24][25]. The Israeli military said it fired on a person in the final stages of preparing an attack in southern Syria [19][23]. The Syrian Foreign Ministry condemned Katz's entry into Syrian territory as a flagrant violation of sovereignty, and al-Shaibani told UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres that regional peace is impossible amid grave violations of national sovereignty [15][26]. Guterres said violations of Syria's sovereignty are unacceptable and must stop [26]. US Special Envoy for Syria Tom Barrack called an Israeli strike on the Abu al-Duhur airbase an unnecessary escalation that does not contribute to regional stability [18][24]. The Turkish Foreign Ministry condemned the airstrike and called on the international community to end Israeli aggression against Syria [18].

Israeli air strikes targeted al-Mansouri, Sarbin, Kafr Rumman, and the Nabatieh district in southern Lebanon, with a large-scale detonation in the town of al-Khiyam [3][6][11][12]. Lebanon's Health Ministry reported 4,350 people killed and 12,310 injured since Israel launched attacks on March 2 [11]. UN spokesperson Stephane Dujarric reported that UNIFIL observed 173 Israeli airspace violations and significant ground and air activity [17]. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun called Israel's aerial herbicide spraying a flagrant violation of Lebanese sovereignty and an environmental and health crime [7]. Dr. Hisham Younes, president of the environmental group Green Southerners, reported a rise in fires coinciding with large-scale Israeli detonations and the demolition of entire neighborhoods, describing the combined tactics as a systematic process of place erasure [7]. Katz stated that all homes in Lebanese border villages will be destroyed, comparing the plan to the demolition of Rafah and Beit Hanoun in Gaza [7]. Political scientist Karim Bitar said Israel's prolonged operation on Ali Taher hill expands the de facto security zone [21].

Israeli forces raided the UNRWA Qalandia Training Centre in occupied East Jerusalem, expelling employees and raising an Israeli flag in the courtyard [2][13]. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the evacuation was conducted in accordance with newly passed anti-UNRWA legislation [13]. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir celebrated the operation on social media, posting "UNRWA in our hands" [2][4]. UNRWA condemned the incursion, stating that the entry of armed security forces into a UN facility without authorization is unacceptable [13]. Roland Friedrich, director of UNRWA affairs in the West Bank, called the break-in a violation of UN privileges and the right of young Palestinians to education [13]. The Palestinian Ministry of Foreign Affairs condemned the seizure as a flagrant violation of international law and the inviolability of UN facilities [13]. Lawyer Mohammad Dahleh described the takeover as a clear violation of international law and the ICJ advisory opinion [13].

Lawmaker Zvi Sukkot used a sledgehammer to destroy a Palestinian memorial in the West Bank village of Madama while under military escort, posting video of the act on Facebook [4][5]. Sukkot stated he took action because the military failed to remove monuments commemorating terrorists, arguing that children who see monuments glorifying killers of Jews will dream of becoming terrorists [4][5]. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich defended Sukkot's position, arguing the state should have dismantled the monument long ago [5]. The Israeli military stated Sukkot acted deceptively and manipulatively, departing from the approved framework, and called the conduct a grave exploitation of security provisions [4][5][8]. Netanyahu condemned taking the law into one's own hands, stating the IDF is the sovereign authority in the West Bank and such actions interfere with counterterrorism missions [4][5][8]. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar said lawmakers are not the sovereign in the West Bank [14]. Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of allowing anarchists like Sukkot to endanger settlement security and damage Israel's international standing [8][14]. Yair Golan, leader of The Democrats party, said Sukkot should be behind bars, not in Israel's Knesset [14]. Rami Nassar, head of Madama's village council, rejected the army's account, saying soldiers stood by as Sukkot vandalised the memorial, and explained the memorial bore names of Palestinians killed in conflicts dating back to 1968 [8][14].

Seven Palestinians were killed in Israeli strikes in Gaza, including three in Beit Lahiya, with a drone strike in al-Mawasi [9]. An Israeli strike destroyed a warehouse storing nutrition supplies for children and pregnant women operated by Ard El Insan and Medical Aid for Palestinians [9]. Sahar Salem, project coordinator at Ard El Insan, said losing the entire nutrition supply stock is a major blow to their work [9]. Dr. Munir al-Bursh, director-general of Gaza's Ministry of Health, warned of a complete collapse of basic necessities, with only 12 of 34 oxygen stations operational [9]. US President Donald Trump said Israel should not be striking in Gaza [9]. Gazan authorities reported nearly 1,300 Palestinians killed by Israeli forces since the October 2025 ceasefire [10].

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar announced the expulsion of Dutch representatives from the International Support Centre for Gaza in Kiryat Gat, giving them one week to leave, in retaliation for the Netherlands' ban on trade in products from Israeli settlements [1][16]. Sa'ar stated that anyone acting against Israel will have no role in the region [1].

Academic analysts characterized Israel's expanding military footprint as reflecting a broader posture. Elizabeth Tsurkov, senior non-resident fellow at the New Lines Institute, said Israel's approach is driven by the collective trauma of October 7 and an assumption that the new Syrian government is hostile [20]. H.A. Hellyer, senior associate fellow at the Royal United Services Institute, described Israel's actions in Syria as an example of Israeli supremacy [20]. Syrian residents recounted detentions and home demolitions: farmer Ahmad Hassan al-Din said his son was taken by Israeli soldiers from their farm near the Golan Heights, and Quneitra resident Mousa Khalil said his house was completely destroyed by Israeli troops [20].

The regional alliance dimension centers on the Mecca Defense Pact. Nasser Hadian, professor of political science at the University of Tehran, said the pact is not directed against Iran in the long term and has limited deterrence effect [22]. Mehdi Zakerian, professor of international relations at Islamic Azad University, argued the pact fits within a historical pattern of Arab and non-Arab states aligning against the Islamic Republic, reinforcing the American-backed regional order [22]. Al-Shaibani said security talks with Israel are expected to resume soon and stressed Syria's sovereign right to determine its alliances [27].

Unresolved: The article mentions Barrack's and Turkey's condemnation of the strike but omits the Israeli Prime Minister's Office claim about Turkish troop deployment and Katz's statement about intelligence, which are available in the cited sources.
factual

Resolved: The article uses one source's naming convention; the discrepancy is minor and does not affect factual accuracy.

The article includes the 1968 detail but not the specific four-Jews claim.
Partially resolved: The article includes the village council's description and Sukkot's general claim about glorifying killers of Jews, but omits Smotrich's more specific characterization.

Borderline formulations

Defensible readings exist on both sides

far-right lawmaker loaded_term (borderline 2/2)
'Far-right' is used in the article's own voice as a descriptor for Zvi Sukkot. While 'far-right' is a commonly used political classification for Sukkot and his Religious Zionism party in mainstream reporting, it is not attributed to any source here and carries an implicit judgment. However, it is a widely accepted factual descriptor of his political positioning. This is a borderline case: it can be read as standard political taxonomy or as a loaded editorial label.
expelling employees and raising an Israeli flag in the courtyard emotionalizing (borderline 2/2)
'Expelling employees and raising an Israeli flag in the courtyard' is the article's own descriptive language of what happened at the UNRWA centre. 'Expelling' could be seen as a loaded verb choice versus a more neutral 'removing' or 'ordering out,' but the article's sources (UNRWA, Palestinian MFA) describe the action in similar terms, and the factual description of raising a flag is straightforward reporting. 'Expelling' is a borderline choice — it could be read as emotionally charged or as an accurate description of forcible removal.
celebrated the operation emotionalizing (borderline 1/2 · cleared 1/2)
'Celebrated the operation' is the article's own characterization of Ben-Gvir's social media post. However, the article immediately quotes his post ('UNRWA in our hands'), which does convey celebration/triumph. The verb 'celebrated' is a reasonable summary of the tone of the post, though a more neutral verb like 'commented on' could have been used. This is a borderline case.

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
