President Donald Trump told US service members in a Thanksgiving video call that "victory over Iran is at hand" and that its timing is his to decide, while US Central Command announced it had disabled a merchant ship in the Gulf of Oman for attempting to breach the American naval blockade of Iranian ports [1][7][12]. Trump said Washington is "giving them every chance to make a deal and save a lot of lives," questioned whether Iranian leaders are "crazy," and vowed that Iran would never acquire a nuclear weapon [1][12][17]. He said he could resume large-scale strikes on underground nuclear sites, including the Pickaxe Mountain facility, if no agreement is reached by year-end, and said Iran has missiles capable of reaching Europe [11][5][14][17].

CENTCOM said a US aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the Palau-flagged Pacific Archer after it departed Iran's Bandar Abbas, disabling its propulsion while leaving the civilian crew unharmed [7][26]. The command said it "continues to strictly enforce the [US] blockade" of Iranian ports [7]. Trump described the blockade as "the greatest naval blockade in history" and said the US has fired 150 missiles to protect commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz [5]. Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baqaei denied any nuclear activity at Mount Pickaxe [5].

Iranian commanders and officials rejected the premise of imminent US victory. IRGC Aerospace Force commander Majid Mousavi said enemies "should expect action in the air and at sea" and that breaking the US blockade is Iran's "firm mission" [24]. Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, said the Strait of Hormuz "will not be opened" and Iran will not allow a blockade to remain in place [24]. Tehran stated it will use "tous les moyens" (all available means) against the blockade [38]. The IRGC announced the explosion of two tankers, while CENTCOM denied that the USS Abraham Lincoln had been targeted [27]. Tehran Friday Prayer Leader Seyyed Ahmad Khatami said Iran has endured 47 years of blockade without the enemy achieving anything and has held the Strait of Hormuz for nine months [18]. Iranian parliamentarian Abbas Golroo said new ways must be designed to neutralize the blockade and that negotiations with Washington are useless unless it accepts Iran's conditions [25].

Analysts argue Tehran is deliberately prolonging the stalemate to deny Trump a diplomatic breakthrough. Iran analyst Vali Nasr said Tehran's strategy is to "`قورباغه را به‌تدریج در آب بجوشانند`" (gradually boil the frog) by sustaining instability in global energy markets, and that Trump is simultaneously pursuing diplomacy, escalation, and blockade without good options [23]. Researcher Ghada Qandil argued Iran can exploit the mismatch between short US political cycles and Iran's multi-layered strategic horizon, though she warned that prolonged economic pressure could reverse that advantage [4]. Iranian officials stated Tehran deliberately prolonged the negotiating stalemate before the US midterm elections to limit Trump's ability to claim a diplomatic breakthrough [4].

The maritime incidents coincided with a drone strike on a Kuwaiti military site that killed two Italian Rheinmetall technicians, producing directly opposed attributions. Kuwait's Deputy Foreign Minister Hamad Al-Mashaan condemned "continued Iranian attacks" and said Kuwait reserves its right to self-defense [7]. Saudi Arabia stated that "continued Iranian attacks further exacerbate tensions and undermine stability," and Gulf Cooperation Council Secretary-General Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi condemned the attack as a dangerous escalation [19][21]. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry called the attack a violation of sovereignty and reserved the right to act under UN Charter Article 51 [20]. Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani announced a formal protest to Iran's embassy and said Gulf countries are not involved in the US war against Iran [21].

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi denied involvement, called the incident "`نسخهای از پیش آمادهشده برای جنگ`" (a pre-prepared script for war), and offered cooperation with Kuwait and Italy to investigate [15][20]. Armed forces spokesperson Abolfazl Shekarchi said Iran conducted no military operation in the Gulf that day and alleged the US and Israel have a history of staging attacks to increase regional insecurity [21]. Commentator Mohammad Marandi raised Ukrainian involvement as a hypothesis before investigations are complete [15].

In Washington, Democratic leaders called for ending the conflict. Presumptive incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries said Congress must "work and move aggressively to end the reckless and costly war of choice in Iran right out of the gate" [3]. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Americans do not know "what the heck we're fighting for in Iran" [3]. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said anyone expecting the midterm outcome to change Washington's policy is "seriously mistaken," and University of Colorado law professor Maryam Jamshidi argued there has been little variation between Republican and Democratic Iran policy [3]. Analyst Negar Mortazavi disputed the equivalence, contrasting Obama's diplomatic breakthrough with Trump's decision to wage war [3].

Third countries framed the crisis through its costs to navigation and commerce. South Korea, which sourced 70 percent of its oil from the Middle East in 2025, is reviewing a motion to broaden its Cheonghae anti-piracy unit's operating area to waters south of the Gulf of Oman, while Foreign Minister Cho Hyun said Seoul does not intend to join any war [6]. Araqchi told Cho that a foreign military presence in the Gulf "would instead heighten tensions and expand the scope of conflict" [6]. Three shipping firms cannot transit the Strait of Hormuz, and Japan's Nikkei warned of a blow to energy supply if the disruption persists [34]. Chinese financial coverage reported that war-risk insurance for the strait is extraordinarily volatile in price [35]. A Lebanese analysis framed the crisis as a "war of two blockades" in which Iran's Hormuz closure and the US counter-blockade impose direct security costs on Gulf states [28]. Brazil's Petrobras is under pressure to revise fuel prices as higher crude prices boost its shares [36].

Russia's Foreign Ministry called the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz inadmissible under humanitarian norms [31]. Pakistan's Defense Minister Khawaja Asif described Iran-US mediation results as fragile and said Pakistan and Qatar continue efforts to revive a June ceasefire [9][22]. Qatar said its contacts with the US and Iran are continuing [39]. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres condemned the Kuwait strike and warned that renewed hostilities "would have devastating consequences for the region and beyond" [7].

Israel's caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is reportedly pressing Trump for a new campaign and wants to preserve the option of striking Iran without US support, while an internal debate pits officials who insist on acting unilaterally against those who oppose going it alone [16][32]. A Pakistani editorial urged Washington to take the military option off the table and Iran to show flexibility, arguing that Israel is the region's only undeclared nuclear power and that US alarmism about Iranian nuclear weapons hardens Tehran [8]. The International Campaign to Abolish Nuclear Weapons reported that the Treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons has 75 states parties, and professor M.V. Ramana said nuclear danger has worsened since 2020, especially through AI and machine learning in nuclear command and control [10].

The mother of one of the Italian technicians killed in Kuwait said the Italian government did not contact the family after her son's death and "ci hanno trattati da cani" (they treated us like dogs) [33]. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky criticized the US for delaying ballistic missile supplies and said the American focus on the Middle East is painful for Ukraine [13]. About six Trump administration cabinet members, including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, have moved into secure military housing amid reported Iranian threats involving dirty bombs or chemical weapons; Bessent said he is "`मैं इन धमकियों से डरने वाला नहीं हूं`" (not afraid of these threats) [2].

Pakistan and Qatar continue mediation efforts to restore a ceasefire, with Asif describing the diplomatic track as fragile and a June agreement having temporarily halted fighting and opened paths on Hormuz shipping and nuclear talks [9][22].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
