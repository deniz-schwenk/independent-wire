Russia Rejects European Role in Ukraine Peace Talks as US Claims Progress

Kremlin insists on trilateral format while European leaders form negotiating team and Germany pledges new air defense funding.

Russia has rejected European participation in the next round of Ukraine peace talks, with Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov stating that no expansion beyond the trilateral Russia-US-Ukraine format has been agreed [13][7]. The rejection followed statements by US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff that Europe would take part, and European Council President Antonio Costa's announcement of an E5-plus-EU negotiating team [13][21]. Moscow separately warned that French troop deployments in Ukraine constitute direct participation in the war [8][16], while Germany pledged $300 million in air defense interceptors for Kyiv and Lithuania's foreign minister called on Europe to ignore Russia's demands [12][17].

Kushner said closed-door discussions are producing constructive ideas and that Washington is preparing a broader multilateral meeting [2][21]. He outlined three negotiation tracks — security guarantees, EU accession, and postwar reconstruction — and said any final agreement must be agreed by both Russia and Ukraine [21]. President Donald Trump said he would discuss Ukraine at a November 19 summit with Xi Jinping and Putin in Shenzhen, calling it his “favourite subject” [1][4][39]. Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning announced the summit as the first face-to-face meeting of the three heads of state [39]. Putin had proposed a trilateral US-China-Russia summit, which Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiga criticized as feeding Putin's self-aggrandizement [38].

Ushakov said the next round will remain trilateral and that European participation would require “clear signals of what constructive contribution” Europe can make [13]. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said European countries are unlikely to secure a relevant role because their declared aim is Russia's strategic defeat, and that Moscow seeks a lasting peace lasting “years, even centuries” rather than a temporary ceasefire [1][10][23]. Lavrov dismissed the European negotiating group as Krylov's animal quartet and described talks with Washington as “ni fu ni fa” [7]. Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov said Russia will achieve its goals politically or militarily and rejected Ukrainian NATO membership as unacceptable [6]. Russia's OSCE envoy Dmitry Polyansky said the OSCE cannot be given any ceasefire-monitoring role, citing the “negative experience” of the Special Monitoring Mission [30]. The OSCE Chairpersonship and Secretary General stated the organization is prepared to monitor a ceasefire once conditions allow [31].

European leaders have formed their negotiating team regardless of Moscow's position. Costa confirmed that the E5-plus-EU team comprises Britain, France, Germany, Italy, Poland and EU institutions [13][19], and Zelenskyy said the team held its first preliminary talks with US and Ukrainian representatives [18]. Zelenskyy said the discussion was “not easy” but produced a result and that participants agreed to meet in Berlin [1][18]. Finnish President Alexander Stubb urged European leaders to engage directly with Putin, calling Europe's reliance on Washington “irresponsible” [22][24]. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said sanctions and frozen Russian assets give the EU leverage and that negotiators must demand concessions from Moscow [29]. German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said sovereign states can choose their security structures and existing alliances cannot be questioned [29].

Lithuanian Foreign Minister Kestutis Budrys called on Europe to “just ignore” Russia's demands to rework Europe's security architecture and concentrate on achieving a ceasefire, followed by troop withdrawal, accountability, and security guarantees for Ukraine [12]. Budrys welcomed the E5-plus-EU format but insisted on pre-briefings, coordination, transparency and trust rather than after-the-fact debriefings [12].

European and Ukrainian officials have questioned whether Moscow is negotiating seriously. Irish Foreign Minister Helen McEntee said Russia has clearly not changed its position [1]. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiga said the Xi-Trump-Putin summit would “only encourage Putin to continue this war” [4]. European diplomats told Politico that Putin is stringing Trump along and that the process restarted in Abu Dhabi may collapse again [15]. Foreign Policy Research Institute fellow Maximilian Hess said Russia's engagement aims to “divide and conquer” [35]. Lavrov accused Europeans of rejecting the Anchorage Putin-Trump agreement and said the US is “again on our side” [9]. Russian polling by the Levada Center found 65% of Russians favor moving to peace talks and 26% favor continuing the war, though the same polling found 59% would prefer intensified strikes if the talks failed [34].

Zelenskyy said US envoys raised the idea of Ukraine withdrawing from Donbas in exchange for NATO membership — an idea he said still lacked specifics — but that Ukraine will not give up its territories and would put any security package to a public vote [6][19]. February 2026 polling by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology found 57% of Ukrainians reject withdrawing from Donbas-held territory in exchange for security guarantees, with support falling further when the format of US guarantees is specified [33].

Lavrov said the deployment of French soldiers to Ukraine for training and maintenance constitutes “direct participation” in the war and that Moscow has warned Paris [8][16]. RFI reported the French “Volia” mission has been present since at least summer 2026 as a small non-combat contingent [16], though RFI's companion report on the mission dates the French presence in Ukraine to at least summer 2016 [27]. French Deputy Defence Minister Alice Rufo rejected Lavrov's characterization as “absurd,” saying France has no intention to fight [8]. President Emmanuel Macron said what is at stake in Ukraine directly concerns France and the security of all [27].

US Under Secretary of Defense Elbridge Colby said Ukraine's deep strikes into Russia, including on civilian infrastructure such as Wildberries, have not shortened the war and may have hardened Russian public resolve and the Kremlin's willingness to escalate [5][28]. German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius called that criticism “quite a stupid thing” and pointed to Russian attacks on Ukrainian energy, water and heating infrastructure [5][8]. British Defence Minister Wes Streeting said Ukrainian strikes have “helped to degrade Putin's war machine, buying time and saving lives” [8]. Pistorius announced a $300 million German allocation for US-made Patriot interceptors for Ukraine under a new PURL package being prepared with Norway [17].

Non-Western actors have advanced parallel mediation tracks. Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan said Russia rejects an interim Black Sea agreement unless tied to a permanent peace deal, while Türkiye continues shuttling between Moscow and Kyiv on grain shipments [20][41]. President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan condemned attacks on civilian vessels in the Black Sea as unacceptable whoever carries them out [40]. India's foreign ministry confirmed it has exchanged food corridor proposals with both Russia and Ukraine as part of a three-part ceasefire proposal [36]. The EU delegation to Bangladesh warned that Russian strikes on Ukraine's export routes threaten Bangladeshi food security, noting over 80% of Bangladesh's wheat is imported [37]. Brazil's President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva offered to help mediate with Russia during a meeting with Zelenskyy [43].

Zelenskyy said Ukraine will propose to US mediators humanitarian missions in 10 Russian-controlled settlements in Kherson and Zaporizhzhia oblasts, modeled on a previous mission in Oleshky, and is preparing a large-scale prisoner exchange and civilian hostage release [14]. Ukraine and Russia have opened a new weekly evacuation channel for vulnerable civilians from occupied territory, with Ukraine's Human Rights Ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets saying 486 Ukrainian women remain in Russian captivity [32].

The next round of talks is being prepared in a trilateral format with no date set [13]. European negotiators plan to meet in Berlin [1]. The Xi-Trump-Putin summit is scheduled for November 19 in Shenzhen [39].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.
civil_society
Non-state organizations representing collective interests (NGOs, human rights groups, trade unions, religious bodies).
government
Executive branch officials, ministries, heads of state, and their spokespersons.
industry
Private companies, trade associations, and commercial actors.
international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.
media
Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
military
Armed forces personnel, commanders, and defense ministries.
