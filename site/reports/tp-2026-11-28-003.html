EU Leaders Fail to Agree on €1.6 Trillion Budget and Ukraine Funding, Postponing Talks to December

A 12-hour Brussels summit exposed divisions over spending cuts, Ukraine aid conditions, and a year-end deadline driven by French elections.

EU leaders ended an extraordinary summit in Brussels without agreement on the 2028-2034 multiannual budget or a €45 billion loan for Ukraine, postponing both issues to a December 17-18 summit [4][15]. The meeting ran approximately 12 hours before leaders canceled the second day, leaving European Council President António Costa acknowledging that no agreement was reached on the long-term budget [13][22]. Costa said he found the summit useful for identifying "the parameters of a possible landing zone in December" and plans a new proposal with €220-230 billion in cuts [18].

Germany's Chancellor Friedrich Merz led the frugal camp demanding deeper reductions, calling the current €1.582 trillion draft insufficient and insisting the EU "can't and we mustn't take on more debt" [4][18]. Merz warned that interest costs of over €150 billion would burden the next seven-year framework and demanded "several hundred billion euros more" in cuts [13][22]. Austria's Chancellor Christian Stocker rejected an agreement "at any price" [13], and the Netherlands' Prime Minister Rob Jetten said Costa must devise a new proposal for more intense December negotiations [4]. On the other side, France's President Emmanuel Macron called the proposed cuts of more than €200 billion a "strategic error" and said Europe was ignoring the Draghi competitiveness report [11][28]. Italy's Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni, who has acted as spokesperson for the Friends of Cohesion group [5], said the EU was "light-years away from a solution" and refused to sign a budget under which Italy pays more and receives less [4][22]. Romanian President Nicușor Dan also represented the group in the negotiations [7]. Greece's Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis warned that excessive cuts would leave no chance to implement Draghi's proposals [28]. Macron has also vowed that "not a single euro of the CAP must be withheld from France in the next European multi-annual budget" [6], and the FNSEA farm union warned that folding the common agricultural policy into a single cohesion fund risks competitive distortions between member states [20]. The NGO confederation CONCORD Europe reported that external action spending was being cut twice as hard as other areas [40].

The €45 billion loan for Ukraine in 2027 — split into €25 billion military and €20 billion civilian components — drew 24 of 27 member states' support, but Hungary, Slovakia, and Czechia refused, and Bulgaria limited its participation to the civilian portion [4][15]. Merz insisted all 27 states must participate, saying "the European Union comprises of 27 member states, not 23 or 24," and threatened to veto the seven-year budget unless they did [2][6]. He stated: "For me, there is a link between our support for Ukraine and the European budget" [25]. Merz has also floated an automatic long-term decision mechanism for Ukraine funding, with decisions by qualified majority — an idea Meloni resists [1][26]. Sweden's Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson said it was "not acceptable to simply kidnap the support for Ukraine by saying that we will accept the European Union support for Ukraine, but we will not pay for it ourselves" [6]. Slovakia's Prime Minister Robert Fico called Merz's approach "a textbook case of blackmail" [3], rejected Costa's draft, and offered bilateral help to Ukraine instead [27]. Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš said Ukraine financing "should be negotiated separately" and that "aid to Ukraine does not belong in the EU budget" [3][31]. Hungary's Prime Minister Péter Magyar opposed the loan and vowed to reject any budget that cuts funds for Hungary or raises its contributions [14][23]. Bulgaria's Prime Minister Rumen Radev said Bulgaria would join the €20 billion non-military portion but contribute "no money for weapons" [4][6].

Macron set December 31 as an absolute limit, warning that after that date he would no longer be "politically or democratically" able to conclude budget negotiations because of France's 2027 presidential election [5][25]. Merz echoed the urgency, noting that elections in multiple member states in 2027 would only harden positions [21]. Ireland's Taoiseach Micheál Martin said prospects for agreement in the first half of 2027 were "very dim" [6].

French presidential frontrunner Marine Le Pen accused Macron and the European Commission of rushing the budget through before the 2027 election to prevent her from defending French interests, calling it "underhanded political manoeuvring to thwart the democratic will of the French people" [2][4]. She vowed that if elected she would slash France's EU contributions and end financial aid to Ukraine [7][25]. Macron accused Le Pen of "strange arrogance" for behaving as if she were already elected [2][39].

Magyar called the summit "useless" and said leaders ended further apart than they began [1][23]. European Parliament Trade Committee chair Bernd Lange mocked the effort as producing "absolutely nothing to show for it" [10]. Frugal governments including Denmark, Sweden, Belgium, and Austria walked out of Costa's small-group negotiation sessions, with Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo described as angry over suspicions the format was meant to divide the frugal camp one by one [8][28][10]. Bruegel economist Jacob Funk Kirkegaard described the breakdown as routine: "Every time there are long-term EU budget negotiations, there are breakdowns, chaos, recrimination and a lot of political theater and drama" [18]. Kirkegaard also noted that net contributors understand that losing the EU's triple-A rating — a risk he tied to a Le Pen government cutting French contributions — would raise their costs [18].

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said EU plans to allocate money for Ukraine in its budget would make Kyiv "less inclined toward a peace process and more determined to continue the war," framing the planned disbursement as "not money for peace" [3][4].

Costa will table a new proposal ahead of the December 17-18 summit, where leaders will attempt to bridge the gaps on both the seven-year budget and the Ukraine loan [1][4]. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen will press for approval of the €45 billion loan at that meeting [1]. European Parliament President Roberta Metsola stressed that the Parliament must approve any deal and wants a 10 percent increase rather than cuts [14].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
