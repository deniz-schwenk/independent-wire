The White House confirmed that Donald Trump, Xi Jinping, and Vladimir Putin will hold the first-ever US-Russia-China trilateral meeting on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen on November 18–19 [3][9]. White House spokesperson Olivia Wales said Trump will discuss reducing nuclear weapons worldwide because he believes proliferation is "very dangerous" [3][4][11]. A senior US official said no agreement is expected and described the meeting as a first, introductory conversation, adding that Trump will raise the war in Iran but not Ukraine, on the grounds that Washington maintains a separate negotiating track with Kyiv and Moscow [3][8][9][11]. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said true arms control in the 21st century is impossible without China because of its rapidly growing nuclear stockpile [9]. Trump said the meeting would be "interesting" and would cover nuclear disarmament and efforts to end the war in Iran [6][14].

China's Foreign Ministry has declined to confirm the trilateral format, saying only that it is "in communication with relevant parties" and will release information when available [2][25][26]. Beijing has previously rejected joining trilateral nuclear disarmament negotiations, calling it "neither fair, reasonable nor realistic" at this stage, given that China's arsenal — estimated at over 600 warheads — remains a fraction of US and Russian stockpiles exceeding 5,000 each [4][17][22]. Russian nuclear expert Alexey Arbatov explained that the US, UK, and France are allies with no interest in constraining one another, while Russia and China voice no mutual concern, leaving a shared ceiling with nothing to anchor it [22]. A CSIS commentary published nine days before the APEC window argued that "denuclearization" is unattainable as a trilateral outcome but that its ambiguity creates room for modest achievements such as missile-launch notifications [23]. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning called the "Chinese spy" narrative surrounding the Nvidia affair a pretext for technology restrictions [24].

The Kremlin, which proposed the meeting [9][21], has described a meeting of the leaders of Russia, China and the United States as one of "extremely great significance" [17]. Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Putin and Trump will discuss "the full range of issues related to the settlement" in Ukraine at APEC, with no Ukrainian or European representatives present [9][18]. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov rejected comparisons with the 1945 Yalta conference, saying no particular vision of world order should be imposed and that arrangements must be based on "mutual benefit and mutual respect" [17]. Putin told schoolchildren at a Moscow exhibition, standing before a sculpture of the Yalta conference, that the leaders of the three main states had gathered to discuss how to organise the world after the war [29]. Analyst Edgar Kagan said Putin's main goal is to create the impression that Russia, China, and the US are making decisions about Ukraine [17].

Ukrainian and European officials have condemned the format. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said the summit would "encourage Putin to continue this war" [9][18]. President Volodymyr Zelensky said he "very much does not want this to become a Yalta-2, 3 or 4" and insisted no US-Russia decisions on Ukraine can be made without Kyiv's participation [18][29]. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz urged Trump to hold talks "without surprises" and to stay on "Team Europe" and "Team Ukraine," warning of a possible "second Anchorage moment" [5][21]. At the European Political Community summit in Dublin, some fifty European leaders expressed concern that their security architecture could be redrawn without them [34]. Polish Sejm Marshal Włodzimierz Czarzasty called excluding Ukraine and the EU "a scandal" [33], and Andriy Kovalenko, former head of Ukraine's Center for Countering Disinformation, compared the proposal to the 1938 Munich agreement, calling it "Munich 2.0 mislabelled as a new Yalta" [35]. Polish Foreign Minister Radosław Sikorski said the first Yalta recognised Soviet dominance over territory the Red Army already controlled, whereas Putin now wants Ukrainian territory he cannot conquer as a gift [35].

Persian-language coverage has framed the meeting primarily through the Iran war. Trump said the US is "trying to end the war in Iran" and has "performed well" in that effort [6][14]. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said a prolonged Tehran-Washington conflict benefits no one and called for dialogue to restore calm in the Middle East and the Gulf [30]. Iranian outlets reported the meeting would focus on arms control and possibly the Iran war and North Korea [6][14].

Japan has formally conveyed concern to Washington about the message the meeting would send [31]. A senior Japanese Foreign Ministry official said that even if the nuclear disarmament discussion can be appreciated to a certain degree, "there is concern about the meeting itself" [31]. Japanese officials expressed scepticism that anything significant would result from the talks, particularly on nuclear cuts [32]. Taipei-based analyst Tseng Wei-feng argued that a three-way meeting including Putin would place extreme pressure on Japan while mattering comparatively less for Taiwan [27].

The Nvidia espionage scandal has shadowed the summit's technology dimension. Former CEO Jensen Huang resigned and agreed to testify before Congress after admitting to an affair with a woman US intelligence reportedly believes was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security [1][24]. Analyst Arthur Kroeber said the scandal "has destroyed the credibility of the biggest corporate lobbyist against US export controls on China" [24], and George Chen predicted the two countries will "start to decouple again in terms of technology" [24]. US officials said the Nvidia affair will not be discussed at the trilateral [3], and Mike Kuiken argued neither Trump nor Xi would gain from raising it [19].

Mexico's President Claudia Sheinbaum said her APEC attendance and possible meeting with Xi pose no risk to ties with Washington, describing Mexico as "friend to all countries" [7]. Zelensky announced after meeting US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner that a major prisoner exchange will occur, European representatives will be more actively involved in diplomacy, and further multilateral meetings are being prepared in the UAE or Switzerland [13]. The Institute for the Study of War said the Kremlin reiterated unchanged demands for Ukraine's complete capitulation and territorial concessions beyond Donbas [13].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
