German and Russian Foreign Ministers Hold First Talks Since 2022 Amid Continued Strikes and Hybrid Attacks in Europe

Wadephul demanded an end to the war and to hostile actions against Europe; Lavrov dismissed the talks and vowed Russia would fight "through to the end."

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul met Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York, the first such encounter between the two countries' top diplomats since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine [7][12][14]. The roughly 20-minute meeting (Politico reported a 30-minute meeting [16]), which Russian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova said was requested by Berlin [3][21], produced no agreement on core points [16][37]. Wadephul described the conversation as intense and said he told Lavrov that Russia must end its war, halt hostile actions against Germany and its allies, and allow Ukrainian grain exports through the Black Sea [12][21]. Lavrov said he heard nothing beyond Berlin's daily public statements and suggested Wadephul could simply have called him [20][37].

Wadephul framed the meeting as a diplomatic duty, telling German television: "Mit Gegnern muss man verhandeln" (One must negotiate with adversaries) [18]. He confronted Lavrov over what Germany considers an attempted drone attack on Leipzig/Halle airport, calling it an unacceptable escalation and warning that Germany can defend itself [8][16]. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha thanked Wadephul for conveying firm shared positions to the Russian side, including the demand that Moscow end its war and warnings against further hostile actions against Europe [3]. Wadephul also pressed for a Black Sea grain export agreement, citing the risk of starvation in the Global South [12][21].

The diplomatic encounter unfolded against continued Russian strikes across Ukraine. Overnight attacks on September 26 involved 173 strike drones, of which Ukrainian air defenses downed 134, with hits recorded in 19 locations [15]. Earlier strikes on Kyiv killed seven people, including a 14-year-old Israeli citizen, and injured 59 [24][30]; the overnight wave killed two women in Zaporizhzhia and two more in Sumy [10][15]. The UN monitoring mission recorded at least 200 civilian deaths in Ukraine in September [24]. Zelensky said Russia is targeting data centres, internet providers and international businesses, including a McDonald's warehouse, disrupting connectivity for about 100,000 households [4][31]. "The Russians are constantly expanding their escalation operation: American and other businesses, data centres, internet providers — for Russia, ordinary life in and of itself is simply a target," Zelensky said [30]. Russia's defence ministry confirmed it hit the Datagroup data centre, which it said was used by Ukrainian military intelligence [4]. Zelensky accused Moscow of seeking any pretext to avoid negotiations and continue the war [1][19].

Lavrov, addressing the UN General Assembly, vowed that Russia's war would continue "through to the end" and that Moscow's military objectives would be achieved regardless of circumstances [2][9]. He accused European countries of doing everything to thwart peace talks the Trump administration is interested in [6][17]. Putin said Moscow would give careful thought to resuming direct peace negotiations but stressed that Ukraine "must feel our retaliatory strikes" [4][24]. Putin rejected warnings of Russian aggression against Europe, saying Russia is not preparing for any kind of combat with Europe and calling European threat warnings a provocation to justify military spending [23].

European leaders described a pattern of Russian hybrid attacks across NATO territory. Danish Defence Minister Jeppe Bruus called the situation "the most serious since the end of the Cold War" [22][23]. Four drones entered Moldovan airspace, two exploding on its territory; two Romanian airspace violations were recorded with one drone crashing near Solca; a fire damaged a Starlink ground station in Poland; and a Russian Mi-8 helicopter violated Polish airspace for 42 seconds [22]. Poland's national prosecutor's office said there was reasonable suspicion the Starlink arson was ordered by Russian special services [4]. A CIA warning relayed by El Mundo flagged possible Russian drone attacks on Italy, France and Spain launched from international waters in the Mediterranean [22][23]. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said Moscow's hybrid attacks try to intimidate Europe into scaling back support for Ukraine, adding: "Europe is doing the opposite" [4]. The EU released €6.6 billion for Ukraine, including military assistance and joint procurement funds [4].

NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe, General Alexus Grynkewich, urged calm, saying the alliance has the tools to manage the risk and is ready to defend every centimeter of its territory [23]. Think-tank analysis notes that Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty does not define "armed attack" — leaving it open whether hybrid or gray-zone incidents could qualify by consensus — and that political risk and consensus requirements would likely block an invocation even after casualties [32][33]. Romanian Defense Minister Radu Miruta said no more than a diplomatic protest was needed in response to the drone that crashed near Solca [22].

Black Sea shipping disruption has driven wheat prices to three-year highs, with Egyptian port arrivals falling about 84% in the first half of September and mills running at 30% capacity [36][41]. Turkey submitted a draft memorandum to Russia and Ukraine to end attacks on civilian and commercial shipping, which Lavrov called unrealistic [40]. Food security expert Caitlin Welsh said the Black Sea is as important for global wheat trade as the Strait of Hormuz is for oil [36]. Bangladesh has seen Ukraine's share of its wheat imports drop from about 19% to 4% [42], and the IMF lists Tanzania, Ivory Coast, Senegal and Mozambique among states heavily dependent on wheat imports from Ukraine and Russia [44]. An AGRA food-security monitor found about 18% of Mozambique's analysed population in acute food insecurity, with conditions expected to deteriorate from October to January [43].

Washington continues to steer both sides toward negotiations. Trump said he hopes Zelensky will "mediate" with Putin [10], while also calling for Kyiv to halt strikes on Russian oil refineries [35]. Zelensky said Trump made a "final decision" to grant Ukraine Patriot production licenses [30], and proposed trilateral technical talks between the US, Ukraine and Russia in the UAE [11]. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said Zelensky could come to Moscow with security guarantees [11]. Ukrainian forces struck two Russian oil refineries in Bashkortostan and the Samara region [5][30], and Ukraine's General Staff confirmed strikes on three facilities involved in missile production [30]. Datagroup said its services continue to operate from backup sites after the Kyiv strike [4].

Russian polling data from the Levada Center shows 68% support for military action and support for peace talks down to 59% [38]. Economists estimate Russian sanctions losses at about 2.9 trillion rubles in 2022-2024, while noting GDP growth exceeded the counterfactual scenario [39]. In occupied Oleshky, Council of Europe Human Rights Commissioner Michael O'Flaherty called for an immediate local ceasefire, describing an alarming humanitarian crisis with no food deliveries since May 26 [25]. Ukraine's human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets said Russia is forcibly displacing and assimilating Ukrainian children, with 1.6 million potentially affected [25]. ArcelorMittal announced the shutdown of its Kryvyi Rih steel plant, Ukraine's largest, after four Russian strikes in five weeks killed five workers [24][28]. Macron cited "several signs" Russia is preparing to mobilize 300,000 people [30].

Wadephul told the UN General Assembly: "I call on every member state to join us in convincing Russia that the future is built at the negotiation table, not on dead bodies in cities and villages" [6]. Lavrov dismissed the meeting's value and offered Russia's readiness to help stabilize the Strait of Hormuz and the wider Gulf region [13][9].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”

Stakeholder types

academia
Researchers, professors, think tanks, and university-based experts.
affected_community
People directly impacted by the events themselves — civilians, displaced persons, local populations. Voices from within the group, not their spokespersons.

international_org
Multilateral bodies and their representatives (UN agencies, IMF, IAEA, Red Cross, regional alliances).
judiciary
Judges, courts, prosecutors, and legal bodies acting in their official capacity.
legislature
Parliament, Congress, or equivalent body. Kept separate from “government” because legislatures often hold positions that differ from their own executive branch.

Journalists, editorial boards, and outlets quoted for their position or analysis, not as sources of factual reporting.
