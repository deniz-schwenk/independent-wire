Netanyahu Frames Flydubai Cockpit Stabbing as Foiled 9/11-Scale Attack as UAE Investigates

Israel pledges to trace the attack to its origin, including possible Iranian involvement, while Flydubai and UAE authorities say the motive remains unconfirmed.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu publicly compared the Flydubai cockpit stabbing to the September 11 attacks [2][41] and pledged that Israel would trace the incident to its origin, including whether Iran was involved [1][6]. The remarks came after a co-pilot stabbed Captain Smit Machchhar aboard flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Tel Aviv, forcing an emergency landing in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia [9][13]. The Boeing 737 Max 8 plunged by differing accounts roughly 14,000 to 17,000 feet — over a span estimated at between about 30 seconds and two minutes — before passengers and off-duty crew subdued the attacker [17][24][13].

Netanyahu told Fox News the co-pilot, identified as an Omani national, had undergone "Islamist radical indoctrination" and was suicidal [6]. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz described the event as "an attempted jihadist terror attack" [9][13]. Israel's foreign ministry briefed diplomats that the incident was "an attempted terrorist attack against Israelis, the United Arab Emirates, and the Abraham Accords" [13]. An unnamed Israeli official said the co-pilot's social media accounts showed jihadist radicalization and discussions of killing Jews [35]. Netanyahu said a vetting loophole had allowed the pilot to fly to Israel and that Israel was addressing it with Emirati cooperation [6][35].

Flydubai stated that "the underlying reasons and motives behind this event are unknown and remain subject to a formal investigation" and urged all parties to "refrain from premature speculation" [9]. The airline confirmed a cockpit altercation, said the aircraft landed safely at Tabuk with all passengers accounted for, and suspended all flights to and from Israel [10][22]. The UAE chief prosecutor, Hamad Saif al-Shamsi, announced an investigation to determine whether the incident was linked to terrorist activity or prior planning [25][29]. Israeli envoy to the UAE Yossi Shelley cautioned that no motive had been determined [30][43], and Israeli security officials said the initial assessment was that the co-pilot likely acted alone with no sign of Tehran's involvement [43]. NBC News reported no indication of Iranian direction, citing US and Israeli officials [33].

US President Donald Trump said Iran would be "hit very hard" if it was deemed to have been involved [6]. Trump told reporters the co-pilot was "perhaps a terrorist or perhaps a whack-job" [9][13], and separately suggested Iran may be connected "based on what I'm hearing" [26]. Netanyahu said it was "too early" to confirm Iranian involvement but that Israel would reach "the origin" of the attack [1][42]. Israeli media raised the prospect of retaliation: Maariv wrote that if a state was found behind the plot it could amount to a declaration of war, and Kan News reported a standing Israeli Air Force plan, prepared after 9/11, for responding to terror attacks using civilian passenger aircraft [14].

Passengers described a sudden descent after the cockpit altercation. Passenger Almog Itali said the plunging aircraft felt like a "rollercoaster" [28]. A passenger told Le Monde of a sudden loss of electrical systems followed by screams of "knife attack, knife attack, blood, blood" [39]. Yaniv Hayun, an Israeli plumber, said he entered the cockpit, put the attacker in a chokehold, and pulled the control column back [13][30]. Dr. Shota Musayev bound the attacker with headphone cords and gave Machchhar first aid [20][29]. Netanyahu, Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi, and Israeli President Isaac Herzog praised the captain and passengers as heroes who prevented a 9/11-scale disaster [6][15][29]. Modi posted on X that Machchhar "showed the world the highest form of courage" and "prevented another 9/11 like attack from happening" [24].

Aviation-security experts noted that post-9/11 protections are designed to keep threats out of the cockpit, not to address insider threats. Jeff Price, a professor of aviation security at Metropolitan State University of Denver, said "everything we did was to try and prevent access to the flight deck, which doesn't do a whole lot of good when the problem is coming from inside the house" [24]. Aviation safety lecturer Oleksandra Molloy said the presence of a weapon on board was "quite concerning" and could lead to stricter security checks for crew and pilots [8]. Israeli PMO spokesperson Doron Spielman said Israel would identify every pilot flying into the country and conduct security checks [25][37]. Israeli Transportation Minister Miri Regev urged Netanyahu to ground Flydubai flights and prioritize Israeli carriers [5].

Jurisdiction over the suspect and the investigation is contested among Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Oman, with Israel seeking a role. The UAE asserts jurisdiction as the state of registry [1][29]. Saudi Arabia held the suspect and transferred him, along with the captain, to Abu Dhabi after medical clearance [6][29]. Oman holds the suspect's nationality, confirmed by an Omani official to CBS News [34]. Netanyahu said UAE President Mohamed bin Zayed agreed Israel could join the interrogation [40]. Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir demanded the suspect be extradited to Israel, though no extradition treaties exist with Saudi Arabia, Oman, or the UAE [35]. Mohamed Mahmoud Mehran, a professor of international law, said jurisdiction is split among Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Oman, and said the Israeli and US political framing must not affect the legal investigation [45].

Opposition figure Yair Lapid cast doubt on any connection between the incident and a pre-election warning Netanyahu had issued the day before, saying a security briefing gave no information about a similar potential incident [13]. Le Monde noted that Netanyahu had warned of possible attacks ahead of the October 27 legislative elections [39]. Al Jazeera reported that Israeli officials were calling the incident a "terror attack" before the airline's investigation was complete [2].

The co-pilot is now in UAE custody, with the UAE prosecutor's investigation underway [29][44]. Flydubai has suspended flights to and from Israel, and Emirates has suspended its codeshare flights with flydubai to and from Tel Aviv [16][22], while El Al announced special rescue flights from Dubai to Tel Aviv [8]. Netanyahu said Israel would know quickly whether Iran was involved [41].

About these labels

Not every tag needs a definition — those listed below cover the full vocabulary used across the dossier.

Divergence types

factual
Sources disagree on a verifiable fact: a date, number, name, or whether something happened.
framing
Sources describe the same event using different language or implied meaning. Example: one outlet calls a payment “compensation,” another calls it “sanctions relief.”
omission
One or more sources report something that other sources leave out entirely.
emphasis
Sources cover the same event but give different aspects different weight or prominence. Example: one outlet leads with casualty figures; another treats them as a footnote to the political negotiations.

Bias issues

evaluative_adjective
A descriptive word that signals the writer’s judgment rather than a neutral fact. Examples: “staggering,” “sharp,” “dramatic.”
intensifier
A word that amplifies a statement without adding information. Examples: “very,” “extremely,” “deeply.”
loaded_term
Vocabulary carrying strong political or emotional connotations that a more neutral word would avoid. Examples: “regime” vs. “government,” “crackdown” vs. “enforcement.”
hedging
Phrases that soften or obscure a claim, making attribution less clear. Examples: “some say,” “allegedly,” “reportedly.”
